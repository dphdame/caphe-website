Two-Year Telehealth Counts Include Earlier Increases in Clinic Use Over Time

Comparing pre-2020 Medicaid biller trends and other care codes with post-2020 counts over 84 months.

In practice today: Medicaid reviews often subtract 2019 provider counts from 2023 counts to judge telehealth pay rules.[1][3] Yet clinics were already adding active billers each year before 2020.[1][2]

What Medicaid records show: Across 84 months of federal records, monthly telehealth-code billers grew from 91,399 in 2019 to 113,638 in 2023 (+24.33%).[1] Yet counts already grew +6,977/yr before 2020 (leaving 2023 -5,669 below trend), while other Medicaid codes grew +18.71%.[2][3]

Policy option: Reviewers can check at least 24 pre-policy months alongside other Medicaid care codes.[1][3] Comparing both groups gives a descriptive +5.62 percentage-point difference in 2023 rather than counting routine growth.[3]

• Recorded Monthly Summary Data. Monthly summaries (telehealth_month_summary.parquet, 168 rows across 84 months and 2 code groups, Jan 2018–Dec 2024) come from the HHS Medicaid Provider Spending file. Provider and code rows report 12-month averages. Paid-dollar rows report 12-month totals.

• Trend Projection Formulas. The straight-line projection adds 6,977 billers each year after 2019 (91,399 + [year − 2019] × 6,977). That formula gives 119,307 in 2023 (-5,669 difference) and 126,284 in 2024 (-24,306 difference; -28,307 on other care from 144,997 vs. 116,690). The percent projection multiplies 91,399 by 1.0826^(year − 2019), giving 125,549 (-11,911) in 2023 and 135,919 (-33,941) in 2024.

• Late-2024 Decline Across Both Groups. Billers on telehealth-eligible codes averaged 108,781 from January through October 2024 (+19.02% vs. 2019). Counts then fell to 88,364 in November 2024 and 47,562 in December 2024. Other Medicaid care fell at the same time, from 124,092 in January–October to 101,476 and 57,878 in November–December. National totals alone cannot prove the cause, though late year-end claim reporting and resumed eligibility checks are both possible reasons.

Why Pre-2020 Trends and Other Care Codes Clarify Two-Year Counts

Subtracting 2019 counts from 2023 counts assumes Medicaid biller counts would have stayed unchanged without new rules. Across 84 months of HHS records, billers on telehealth codes rose by +22,239 (+24.33%) from 2019 to 2023. Monthly averages moved from 91,399 to 113,638. Yet two wider changes happened at the same time. First, telehealth codes had already added +6,977 billers (+8.26%, from 84,422 to 91,399) from 2018 to 2019. Second, while renewals were paused, billers on other Medicaid codes rose by +18.71% (109,087 to 129,492).

Checking both pre-2020 trends and other care codes avoids two opposite budget errors. Extending the one-year 2018-2019 gain straight to 119,307 leaves 2023 counts 5,669 billers below trend. That difference shows how strongly one pre-policy year affects a five-year projection. By contrast, subtracting growth in other Medicaid care leaves a descriptive +5.62 percentage-point difference through 2023. Late-2024 drops (-24,306 and -28,307 vs. trend) also happened in both code groups at once. Late claim reports or coverage checks may explain that shared decline.

Common Questions and Study Notes

Why does a two-year before-and-after count overstate policy gains when clinic staffing is already rising?
A two-year count assumes the 2019 average of 91,399 billers would not have changed without the rule. Because clinics were already adding staff, that subtraction counts routine clinic and enrollment growth as a policy gain.
What do the 2018-2019 growth rate (+8.26%) and other care (+18.71%) show about the +24.33% telehealth increase?
Telehealth-eligible codes were already adding +6,977 billers per year (+8.26%) before 2020. Other Medicaid care also grew +18.71% from 2019 to 2023 while coverage renewals were paused. Together, these figures show that most of the +24.33% increase occurred alongside wider Medicaid growth.
Why did both telehealth-eligible and other Medicaid biller counts fall in late 2024?
Through October 2024, billers on telehealth-eligible codes averaged 108,781 (+19.02% above 2019). Counts then fell in November and December 2024 for both telehealth-eligible codes (88,364 and 47,562) and other care (101,476 and 57,878). National totals alone cannot prove the cause. Late claim reporting and resumed eligibility checks are both possible reasons for that shared decline.
