Programs With Large Long-Term Health Gains Still Need Plans for Yearly Cash and Reserves

Comparing multi-year health value, if causal, with Year-1 cash costs, state reserve goals, and budget rules.

In practice today: Long-term health gains do not add cash to this year's budget.[1] Still, funding requests often cite multi-year health value with no Year-1 cash or reserve plan.[2]

What the comparison shows: High long-term value still faces Year-1 cash limits.[1][2] A modeled 12-week leave plan lowers 2026 reserves to 10.8% ($1.96B) vs. EDD's 25% ($4.38B) goal, and the FY 2026–27 state budget sets $709M for county staff with $0 for CBO grants.[2][3]

Policy option: Budget teams can check two numbers side by side.[2] Multi-year health value shows program worth, while Year-1 cash and reserve plans match the right payer.[2][3]

Why Program Reviews Need Both Qualified Value Estimates and a Yearly Cash Plan

Proposals citing large social value cannot move forward when they leave out a Budget Impact Analysis. County health links correspond to 32:1 in Year 1 and 59:1 over 13 years (adj. p = 0.067) as illustrative resident value if causal, not cash returns. Paid Family Leave (PFL) plans estimate $422.0M in net worker pay, and Medi-Cal outreach keeps coverage for $35 to $140 per member-year. Yet budget offices must verify Year-1 cash costs, EDD reserve goals, and state payroll tax law (Cal. Unemp. Ins. Code § 984). Resident health value does not place cash into agency accounts.

Reporting both tables side by side gives lawmakers a complete budget plan. A 12-week PFL plan requires $623.7M in 2026 gross State Disability Insurance cash. That cost lowers reserves to 10.8% ($1.96B), below EDD's 25.0% ($4.38B) planning goal (not a legal minimum). At the 1.300% tax rate, wage growth across all covered jobs raises reserves to 30.4% ($6.37B) by 2030. To reach EDD's goal sooner, lawmakers can set a 1.345% rate under § 984 (below the 1.5% cap). For Medi-Cal, $709M in state funds pays only county staff, so CBO teams partner with health plans.

Common Questions and Study Notes

How does a Budget Impact Analysis differ from cost-effectiveness or cost-benefit studies?
Cost-effectiveness and cost-benefit studies compare total health or resident value per dollar across whole populations. By contrast, a Budget Impact Analysis estimates yearly gross cash spending, revenue, and EDD reserve goals for one payer. It also checks legal budget or tax authority over one to five years.
Why must the State Disability Insurance fund budget $623.7M in cash when net worker pay rises by $422.0M?
Many private employers coordinate paid leave with state Paid Family Leave (modeled at 30%). State checks then replace part of employer-paid leave, reducing added worker pay from $602.8M gross benefits to $422.0M net. Even so, the state SDI fund must still pay the full $623.7M cash bill.
How do the yearly and 13-year county health death links compare in conditional VSL terms?
Across 57 California counties before 2020, the one-year link (2.38 fewer deaths per 100,000 per $10) equals 55% of the 13-year link (-4.32 per 100,000, adj. p = 0.067). At the $13.6M federal Value of a Statistical Life (VSL), which values small reductions in death risk, those links equal $420,168 per statistical life (32:1) and $231,481 per statistical life (59:1). Both figures represent illustrative resident value if causal, not cash paid to counties.
