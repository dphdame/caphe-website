Fixed Medi-Cal Visit Fees Pay Less Than One Tenth of County Care Worker Costs

Comparing fixed per-visit fees with county worker wages, hospital stay savings, and two-year health gains.

Community Health Workers (CHWs), including workers known as Promotoras, help low-income families manage illness and avoid emergency room (ER) visits. Medi-Cal pays a fixed per-visit fee for scheduled clinic teaching sessions across the state. Yet outreach workers spend most of each week driving to homes and helping families get food, housing, and care. Because Medi-Cal does not pay for travel time, hiring counties pay full worker costs up front. Meanwhile, lower hospital costs benefit separate health plan and hospital budgets.

Medi-Cal visit fees cover only 6.5% to 9.8% of full costs for hospital outreach workers.[1][2] Full worker cost includes pay, benefits, training, and supervision, and runs $92,684 to $122,102 per year.[1][2] Meanwhile, Medi-Cal pays a fixed $31.74 per 30-minute group session across all 58 counties.[1] Even so, modeled first-year savings reach ~$107,000 in plan claims or ~$182,000 to $291,000 in bed costs.[2][3] In those three models, first-year savings exceed worker cost.[2][3] Local outreach adds ~8.2 Quality-Adjusted Life Years (QALYs) over two years, scored from 0 for death to 1 for full health.[3][4] Subtracting first-year hospital savings leaves a net outlay of ~$7,800 per QALY.[3][4] Tracking fees, bed savings, and QALYs apart matches each team to a payer.[3][4]

• Why Fixed Medi-Cal Fees Leave a Pay Gap: Under DHCS SPA 22-0001, Medi-Cal pays $31.74 per 30-minute group session and $26.66 per individual session in all 58 counties. Those fees do not adjust for local wages. Meanwhile, 2024 BLS wage data put full worker cost at $103,719 statewide ($92,684 rural to $122,102 Bay Area). That total includes a $54,000 base salary, 35% benefits, $19,000 supervision, $4,000 training, and 10% shared expenses.

• Health Plan Claims vs. Full Hospital Costs: Scenario 1 values avoided care using Medi-Cal plan payment rates ($600 per ER visit, $7,000 per stay, $6,000 per return stay). Those figures show what a health plan saves in paid claims. Scenarios 2 to 4 use full hospital costs ($2,100 per ER visit, $18,500 per stay, $15,000 per return stay) from HCAI and AHRQ HCUP. Those figures measure fewer occupied beds and less unpaid hospital care.

• Why Neighborhood Outreach Needs a Two-Year View: State 2024 HCAI hospital data report 279,905 adult stays that timely care may help prevent (902.5 per 100,000 overall in PQI 90; 715.3 chronic in PQI 92). In areas where 60% of homes speak a non-English language, 13.7% of immigrant adults delay care (2.39 times the odds in Wolstein et al., 2022). An assumed 150-adult Promotora group avoids 6.6 ER visits and 1.10 stays in Year 1 ($34,900 saved). That worker also completes 38 early health checks per year and adds 8.18 QALYs over two years. Subtracting $34,900 from the $98,469 worker cost leaves $63,569, or $7,771 per QALY, which is 92% below $100,000 per QALY (Neumann et al., 2014).

Matching CHW Funding to the Budget That Receives the Savings

County leaders face a budget mismatch when starting a CHW care team. The hiring agency pays full worker costs of $92,684 to $122,102 per year, including pay, benefits, training, and supervision. Yet Medi-Cal pays a fixed $31.74 fee only for scheduled 30-minute sessions. Because home travel and phone calls fill most work hours, visit fees cover less than one-tenth of hospital outreach team costs. Judging every team only by Year-1 hospital bills also leaves out two-year local health gains.

Separating visit fees, Year-1 hospital savings, and two-year QALYs matches each team to a payer. Hospital teams with 55 assumed patients save ~$107,000 in plan claims or ~$291,000 in bed costs in Year 1. So monthly health plan pay or hospital funds can cover those worker costs. Clinic teams with 75 assumed patients earn ~$46,600 in visit fees and save ~$182,000 in hospital costs. Meanwhile, neighborhood Promotoras serving 150 assumed adults add ~8.2 QALYs over two years at ~$7,800 per QALY. That low cost per healthy year supports steady public health grants for local outreach.

How the Model Works and Where Its Data Come From: This model combines state Medi-Cal fee rules, 2024 HCAI hospital counts, 2026 DHCS enrollment counts, and 2024 BLS wages in 58 counties. It pairs those state records with published results from random-assignment trials and two-year health scores. Each column models one full-time CHW across an assumed yearly caseload of 55, 75, or 150 people.

State Payment Rules (No Local Wage Adjustment): California DHCS SPA 22-0001 sets fixed statewide pay for patient education codes CPT 98960-98962. Medi-Cal pays $26.66 per 30-minute one-on-one session and $31.74 per group session with billing label U2. Fees do not change for county wages or rent. Paid visit counts, avoided stays, QALYs, and net costs are modeled estimates.

58-County Hospital and Worker Cost Records: Preventable stay rates come from the 2024 HCAI Prevention Quality Indicators across 31,012,717 adults. Those files list 279,905 preventable stays overall (902.5 per 100,000) and 221,844 stays for chronic conditions (715.3 per 100,000). County pay comes from 2024 BLS wage data ($103,719 median full worker cost, from $92,684 to $122,102). Full cost includes a $54,000 base salary, benefits equal to 35% of salary, supervision, training, and 10% shared costs.

Trial Effects and Two-Year Health Scores: Modeled care reductions (-22% ER visits, -30% stays, -23% return stays) come from the IMPaCT trials (Kangovi et al., 2020). Health is scored from 0 for death to 1 for full health. Weighted gains for chronic illness (+0.025), mental health (+0.040), and social needs (+0.020) give 0.0545 QALYs per adult over two years at 3% discounting. Results are compared with $100,000 per QALY (Neumann et al., 2014) and UCSF Healthforce Center surveys (N = 210).

Common Questions and Study Notes

When should a county use each of the four Community Health Worker care models?
Each model fits a different county goal and payer. Hospital teams (55 assumed patients) fit plans or hospitals seeking Year-1 savings of ~$107,000 in claims or ~$291,000 in bed costs. Clinic teams (75 assumed patients) fit primary care sites that bill ~$46,600 in scheduled visits while reducing hospital stay costs by ~$182,000. Neighborhood Promotoras (150 assumed adults) fit public health teams serving families outside clinic care, adding ~8.2 QALYs at ~$7,800 per QALY.
Why do Year-1 hospital bills alone understate the value of neighborhood Promotora outreach?
Promotoras reach families early, before problems from high blood sugar or blood pressure require emergency care. Because most adults in the group do not need acute hospital care right away, Year-1 hospital savings equal $34,900. Yet over two years, outreach completes ~38 early health checks per year and adds ~8.2 QALYs across 150 assumed adults. Subtracting $34,900 from the $98,469 full worker cost leaves ~$7,800 per QALY, or 92% below the $100,000 benchmark.
Does Medi-Cal raise its per-visit CHW fee in high-wage California counties?
No, Medi-Cal pays the same fixed rate in every county. State rules pay $31.74 per 30-minute group session and $26.66 per one-on-one session statewide. Meanwhile, full worker costs including pay, benefits, training, and supervision range from $92,684 in rural counties to $122,102 in the Bay Area. As a result, home visit billing covers only 6.5% to 9.8% of hospital team costs, so counties pair fees with monthly plan contracts or hospital funds.
