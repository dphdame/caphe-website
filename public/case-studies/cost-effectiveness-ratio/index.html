Five-Year Contracts Divide First-Year Care Team Setup Costs Across Five Years of Care

Comparing Year-1 hiring and setup costs with five-year ongoing and full costs per healthy year gained.

Judging a new care team on Year 1 alone charges all first-year hiring, training, and setup costs to one year of patients. Starting a 10-worker county Community Health Worker (CHW) team costs $1,930,000 in Year 1 ($1,825,000 direct pay plus $105,000 setup). In Years 2 to 5, yearly cost falls to $1,405,000 ($1,350,000 at $1,800 per client plus $55,000 setup). Five-year contracts divide setup costs across five years of Quality-Adjusted Life Years (QALYs). One QALY equals one year in full health.

Dividing first-year setup costs across five years lowers cost per healthy year gained.[1][2] Serving 750 clients per year brings 40.04 QALYs per yearly group and 200.20 QALYs across five groups.[1] Five-year direct cost totals $7,225,000 ($36,089 / QALY).[1] Full 5-year cost with $325,000 setup totals $7,550,000 ($37,712 / QALY).[1] Checked on Year 1 alone ($1,930,000), cost reaches $48,202 / QALY.[2] In Years 2-5, cost falls to $1,405,000/yr ($35,090 / QALY, with $55,000/yr setup).[2] Five-year net value is +$2.46M with setup at $50,000/QALY and +$12.80M direct (+$12.47M with setup) at $100,000/QALY.[3] Moving from 5-6 CHWs to 10 CHWs costs $50,339 per added QALY.[3]

Why Five-Year Contracts and Net Dollar Value Clarify Cost-per-QALY Reviews

Checking a single Year-1 ratio ($48,202 / QALY) or 5-year ratio ($36,089 / QALY direct; $37,712 / QALY with setup) leaves two questions open. First, why does Year 1 cost 37% more per QALY than Years 2 to 5 ($35,090 / QALY)? Second, how can counties compare a 10-worker team with a smaller team when ratios omit total QALYs? A 5-year contract divides first-year hiring and setup across all 200.20 QALYs. U.S. benchmarks ($50,000, $100,000, and $150,000 per QALY) also turn healthy years into net dollars.

Subtracting gross 5-year costs ($7,225,000 direct; $7,550,000 with $325,000 setup) from valued QALYs gives one net sum. At the $100,000 per QALY benchmark, the 750-client-per-year CHW team brings +$12.80M in direct net value. Including all $325,000 in setup costs still leaves +$12.47M in net value. Even at the $50,000 benchmark, 5-year net value stays above zero (+$2.79M direct; +$2.46M with setup) before any avoided hospital stays.

Common Questions and Study Notes

What is the difference between a "cost-effective" program and a "cost-saving" program?
A program is cost-effective when its cost per QALY ($36,089 direct operating or $37,712 with setup) is below the chosen value benchmark ($50,000 to $150,000). A program is cost-saving only when avoided hospital bills exceed program costs, so total cash spending falls while health improves.
Why do health economists report net dollar value alongside cost per QALY?
A cost-per-QALY ratio divides added cost by added QALYs, which leaves out total program size. A 20-patient pilot can have the same ratio as a 750-patient team. Net dollar value multiplies QALYs by the benchmark value and subtracts program costs, giving one total (+$12.80M operating at $100,000/QALY).
How much do Year-1 setup costs change the 5-year cost per QALY?
Reviewed on Year 1 alone, initial hiring and setup bring cost to $48,202 / QALY ($1,930,000 for 40.04 QALYs). Divided across five years (200.20 QALYs), $325,000 in setup costs adds $1,623 per QALY. Five-year cost rises from $36,089 / QALY ($7,225,000 direct operating) to $37,712 / QALY ($7,550,000 full cost).
