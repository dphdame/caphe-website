Matching Paid Medicaid Claims With Federal and State Ban Lists Finds Providers One List Leaves Out

Matching paid Medicaid claims with federal and state ban lists to find state-only bans and 50-month waits.

Health plans check clinic lists and ban lists to count doctors and block banned billers. Yet federal and state ban lists do not update each other, so their records differ. Fraud cases also take years to close after a biller sends a final paid claim. So formal bans often post long after billing ends. Checking one list on one date leaves out state-only bans and misses past claims.

Health and Human Services (HHS) files record 488,032 national Medicaid billers from 2018 to 2024.[1] Matching paid claims with federal OIG and state ban lists finds 487,384 billers with no match.[1][2] The remaining 648 matched billers split into three groups.[1][2] They include 299 bans under federal fraud rules, 199 on the California list only, and 150 other federal bans.[1][2] Checking the federal list alone leaves out all 199 state-only records (30.7%).[2] Meanwhile, 95 billers banned under fraud rules in 2025 or later received a median $9,694.90 per month.[3] That monthly paid amount exceeds comparison billers ($3,820.94) and 204 billers banned in 2018 to 2024 ($6,721.78).[1][3] The median time from final paid claim to federal ban was a 50.0-month wait.[3]

• Matching Counts Across Ban Lists. Out of 488,032 Medicaid billing IDs (487,384 with no match plus 648 matched), the 648 matched providers include 299 federal exclusions under fraud-related rules (204 dated 2018-2024 and 95 dated 2025 or later under Social Security Act sections 1128(a)(1), 1128(a)(3), and 1128(b)(1)). They also include 199 California-only suspensions (30.7%) and 150 other federal exclusions (449 federal matches in total, or 69.3%).

• Monthly Payments and Wait Until a Ban. Median monthly payments are 1.76 times as high as comparison billers for 2018-2024 fraud-related bans ($6,721.78 vs. $3,820.94) and 2.54 times as high for 2025+ bans ($9,694.90 vs. $3,820.94). The 50.0-month median wait (average 50.7; middle half 32.5 to 68.0 months) measures time from the final paid claim to a federal ban. Matching updated lists corrects past records, while plans still need to watch current claims.

• Separate Findings on Provider Directories. Zhu et al. (2023, Health Affairs) found that 58% of listed Oregon Medicaid mental health providers saw few or no Medicaid patients. That finding shows why plans should check paid claims alongside provider lists.

Matching Paid Claims With Both Federal and State Ban Lists

Clinic lists and ban lists show records from one date. They show who held a license or ban that day, not who billed for care. Across 488,032 Medicaid billers and 648 matched billers, one list leaves out two facts. First, clinic lists keep doctors who see few or no Medicaid patients (58% in Oregon). Second, federal OIG checks leave out 199 billers on the state list (30.7% of 648).

Checking both ban lists still shows closed cases rather than fraud occurring now. Formal bans often post years after billing stops. Here, 95 billers banned under fraud rules in 2025 or later received $9,694.90 per month (2.54x the $3,820.94 median). Among those 95 billers, 90.5% stopped billing before 2024. Their federal bans posted a median 50.0-month wait after their final paid claim. Matching monthly paid claims with both lists helps plans count care and check past bills.

Common Questions and Study Notes

Why does checking only the federal OIG ban list miss suspended Medi-Cal billers?
State Medicaid rules let states suspend billers without updating the federal OIG list right away. As a result, state and federal files can list different billers. Out of 648 matched Medicaid billers in the 2018 to 2024 file, 199 (30.7%) appear on the California list only, with zero federal match.
Why do billers later banned for fraud have no recorded ban in checks from the billing year?
Fraud cases and formal ban orders take years to close after a biller stops filing claims. Here, the median wait from the final paid claim to a federal ban is 50.0 months. Among 95 billers banned in 2025 or later, 90.5% stopped paid claims before 2024. While billing, they received a median $9,694.90 per month.
What steps should health plans and audit teams use to check provider lists?
Plans should match every billing ID each month against paid claims, the federal OIG list, and state ban files. Next, teams should count care access using paid claims and patients, not names on a clinic list. Finally, staff should match new ban lists to older claims when checking past bills.
