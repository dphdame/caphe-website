Distinct Healthy-Year and Death-Risk Units Help Counties Compare Care and Prevention

Checking clinic and mental health care in healthy years while keeping death-risk values apart.

In practice today: Diabetes clinics track lab tests, mental health teams score mood symptoms, and counties count death rates.[1][3] Without shared units, mental health and social support often drop out of budget reviews.[2]

What our model estimates: Suppose Community Health Workers serve 750 clients per year across chronic, mental, and social care.[1][2] Two-year gains reach 40.04 QALYs ($4.00M at $100K/QALY; 54.5% mental and social), kept separate from 32:1 yearly and 59:1 13-year county death-risk values if causal (adj. p = 0.067).[2][3]

Policy option: Counties can assign one main care group per client so mental and social gains count once.[2] Reporting QALYs, death-risk values (if causal), and hospital cash savings in separate rows stops double-counting.[1][3]

How Separate Health Units Help Counties Compare Mental Health and Clinic Care

Judging programs only by single lab tests favors care tied to one blood test or code. Mental health help, housing aid, and county health work are then left out of budget reviews. Without a shared unit, budget teams cannot compare mood symptom relief for 225 Medi-Cal members with blood-sugar gains for 375 patients.

Shared health units allow fair checks while keeping distinct measures apart. Across 750 clients served by CHWs, two-year gains total 40.04 QALYs per year, worth $4,004,083 at $100,000 per QALY. Mental health (17.49 QALYs) and social help (4.34 QALYs) make up $2,183,037 (54.5%). Chronic care makes up the other $1,821,046 (18.21 QALYs). In a separate unit, HHS Value of a Statistical Life (VSL) rates ($6.3M, $13.6M, and $20.7M) value death-risk cuts. In that unit, county health links equal 32:1 yearly and 59:1 at 13 years (adj. p = 0.067). Both show resident value if causal, not cash returns.

Common Questions and Study Notes

What does a Quality-Adjusted Life Year (QALY) measure, and why is it discounted at 3%?
One QALY equals one year lived in full health on a 0-to-1 scale, where 0.00 is death and 1.00 is full health. Second-year health gains are discounted by 3.0% per year to express two-year gains in present-year terms. Across the 750-client cohort, discounted two-year gains average 0.0534 QALYs per client, or 40.04 QALYs in total.
Why must analysts never add QALY dollar values and Value of a Statistical Life (VSL) figures together?
QALYs ($50,000 to $150,000 per QALY) measure daily health quality and length of life in clinic studies. By contrast, federal VSL ($6.3M to $20.7M per statistical life) values small reductions in population death risk in policy studies. Adding both on the same group counts longer life twice.
How does assigning one main care group per patient prevent double-counting?
Adding separate diabetes and depression gains for the same patient can overstate total health improvement. Instead, each person in the 750-client cohort is counted in one primary care group (50% chronic, 30% mental, 20% social), or measured with one combined health survey.
