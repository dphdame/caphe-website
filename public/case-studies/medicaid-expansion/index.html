In the Model, Most Medi-Cal Renewal Losses Come From Unreturned Mail Forms Over Work Rules

The model separates adults who miss mail forms from adults who do not meet work rules.

Upcoming rule in 2027: Federal H.R. 1 rules will make 120,239 county adult Medi-Cal members prove work hours or an exemption every six months.[1] Members get paper forms in the mail whenever state wage files do not confirm status.[1][2]

What our model estimates: In Census data, 88.5% of those adults work or hold an exemption.[2] Still, if half of paper forms go unreturned, 27,076 adults lose care per cycle, and 52.5% (14,219) are eligible members who missed the form.[2][3]

Policy option: Pre-filling exemptions from clinic and school records would keep eligible caregivers and students covered.[3] Adding a six-month hour check for gig workers would prevent 71.4% (10,150) of missed-form losses.[3]

• Separating Earlier Decline From New Rules. Observed county expansion counts fell 12.31% before H.R. 1 work checks began (14,803 fewer adults, from 120,239 in June 2025 to 105,436 in June 2026 in CHHS Open Data). Studies must separate this earlier decline before estimating losses from the new rules.

• Why More Adults Get Paper Forms in Low-Jobless Counties. State estimates exempt 373,389 adults in high-unemployment counties before Step 5 mailings (dhcs_verification_funnel_scc.csv:5). At ~4.1% unemployment, Santa Clara gets no area exemption (0.0% share). Thus, 9,689 more adults face Step 5 paper checks (82,126 vs. 72,437 under state rates). At a 50% loss rate, predicted losses rise from 36,218 to 41,100 per period.

• Added Effect of Phone Uploads. In the model, phone uploads prevent 1,423 more losses per period on the state count. That step raises prevented losses from 14,219 to 15,642 of 27,076 (57.8%). On the Census base, summing the eight survey groups gives 17,350 of 30,007 (57.8%). Scaling the four-group total directly to 133,998 gives 30,174 losses.

Scenario Calculations and Comparison of State and Census Counts
Expand scenario notes

• Scenario 1 Baseline Paper Count. On the state count of 120,239 adults, state files do not confirm 54,152 adults. Those members get paper forms, and at a 50.0% loss rate, 27,076 lose coverage per period (vs. 41,100 under state rates).

• Scenario 2 Losses Prevented in Two Groups. Clinic and school record checks help 6,831 exempt adults keep care (1,241 caregivers, 2,295 disabled or new parents, and 3,295 students). Six-month hour checks add 3,319 gig workers, for 10,150 prevented losses (37.5% of 27,076, or 71.4% of 14,219).

• Scenario 3 Full Plan and Survey Base. Adding W-2 paystub links (+2,228) and clinic health checks (+1,841) reaches 14,219 (52.5%). Phone uploads add +1,423 for 15,642 of 27,076 (57.8%). On the Census base, summing eight survey groups gives 17,350 of 30,007 (57.8%), while scaling to 133,998 gives 30,174.

Why Counties Need Different Renewal Actions for Each Adult Group

A single county estimate of 36,000 to 41,000 losses does not show why members lose coverage. That total mixes adults who no longer qualify with adults who miss mail forms. If leaders assume all exits reflect lost eligibility, clinics may cut care teams too soon. Uninsured adults who still qualify may then visit county ERs, though the model does not count ER visits.

Splitting renewals into four work and exemption groups guides county staff time. Across 120,239 observed expansion adults, 88.5% work 80+ hours, have an exemption, or work part-time. Yet 71.4% of modeled missed-form losses (10,150 of 14,219) fall in two groups outside wage files. Caregivers, disabled adults, and students account for 6,831 avoidable losses, and gig workers add 3,319. Filling in clinic and school exemptions, checking six-month hours, and texting upload links keeps members covered.

Common Questions and Study Notes

What share of adults in Santa Clara County's Medi-Cal expansion already work or qualify for an exemption?
Nearly nine in ten adults in this expansion group work or qualify for an exemption in Census survey estimates. Adjusted to the observed count of 120,239 members, 38.0% work at least 80 hours per month. Another 39.5% qualify for a parent, disability, or student exemption, reaching 77.5% across both groups. Gig and part-time workers add 11.0% (88.5% combined). For 11.5% (13,767 adults), survey records show neither paid work nor an exemption.
Why do more adults receive paper renewal forms in a low-unemployment county than the state average?
Low county unemployment prevents the countywide exemption used in high-unemployment areas. State estimates exempt 373,389 adults in high-unemployment counties before mailing paper forms. Because Santa Clara County has about 4.1% unemployment, zero county members receive that area exemption. Because of that rule, 9,689 more county adults must complete paper checks. If half do not return forms, predicted losses based on state rates increase from 36,218 to 41,100 per period.
Which Medi-Cal adult groups face the most avoidable coverage losses from unreturned forms?
Caregivers, adults with disabilities, and college students face the most avoidable losses in the model. The model predicts 6,831 avoidable losses in that group per six-month period, or 72.4% of its total losses. Gig and part-time workers rank second with 3,319 modeled avoidable losses, or 64.6% of their losses. Both groups face high form loss rates because employer wage files lack school, care, and cash work records.
