Minimum Group Sizes and Net Member Losses Help Counties Set Medi-Cal Call Lists

Using a 100-member minimum and net losses across 62 Medi-Cal groups to set call lists.

County Medi-Cal teams check monthly Department of Health Care Services (DHCS) totals across 62 age, aid, and language groups. Sorting all 62 groups by percent loss alone puts very small groups first. A net loss of 13 to 17 members cuts a 50-person group by 25% to 34%. Running dozens of raw 5% tests on survey data can also mark random gaps as real. A 100-member minimum plus losses past the county rate keeps call lists on the largest declines.

A 100-member minimum limits noisy percent ranks, and losses past the county rate show large drops.[1][2] From June 2025 to June 2026, Medi-Cal counts across 62 DHCS groups fell from 472,014 to 411,722 (-60,292 net, or -12.77%).[1] Percent rank puts small groups first: Samoan at -34.04% (-16), French at -25.00% (-14), and Thai at -20.75% (-11).[1] Monthly overlapping totals cannot support a simple exit test.[1] When error is measured, multi-test rules cut false positives in survey areas (p = 0.469 to 0.730), 16-delay checks (adj. p = 0.0788), and 1,000 label swaps (0.593 max chance score).[2][3] Staff refer groups under 100 members to community-based organizations (CBOs).[1] Ranking 100+ groups by losses past -12.77% puts Spanish first (-24,880 net, 41.27% of county loss, -7,230 extra).[1] Overlapping groups ages 19-44 follow (Hispanic -17,380 or 28.83%, -6,344 extra; Men -15,496, -5,697 extra).[1]

• 62-Group DHCS Count Rules & Why Simple Exit Tests Fail: Santa Clara DHCS tables cover 62 overlapping age, aid, and language groups from June 2025 (472,014) to June 2026 (411,722; net change -60,292 or -12.773%, rounded to -12.77%). Extra loss past -12.77% is -7,230 for Spanish (-24,880 vs. -17,650 expected), -6,344 for Hispanic 19-44, and -5,697 for Men 19-44. Small-group values are -10 for Samoan, -6.8 for French, and -4.2 for Thai (math differences, not whole people). Monthly tables show net change (entries minus exits), count the same people across rows, and group families together. Because single exits are unseen, simple exit p-values do not apply.

• Multiple-Test Error Risk & CAPHE Checks: If 62 tests have no true gap, a raw 0.05 rule gives 62 × 0.05 = 3.10 false positives on average. Bonferroni divides 0.05 by 62 (0.00081). Where CAPHE studies measure error, three checks cut false findings. First, pair tests across the top three Santa Clara Census areas (80 weights) give p = 0.469 to 0.730, so their order is unclear. Second, across 16 spending delays, a 4-year raw p = .0049 adjusts to 0.0788 under Holm and false discovery rate (FDR) rules. Third, across 1,000 random-label tests in the billing study, the max chance score is 0.593 versus 0.724 on real labels (p = 0.001).

How Minimum Group Sizes, Multiple-Test Rules, and Excess Loss Guide Call Lists

Checking 62 Medi-Cal groups in Santa Clara County (472,014 to 411,722 members, or -60,292 and -12.77%) shows two ranking errors. First, ranking by percent loss alone puts small groups first: Samoan (-34.04%, -16), French (-25.00%, -14), and Thai (-20.75%, -11). Meanwhile, Spanish ranks 11th by percent (-18.00%) yet makes up 41.27% (-24,880) of county loss. Second, if 62 tested gaps are all zero, a raw 5% rule gives 3.10 false positives on average. In studies that support tests, 16-delay checks move from raw p = 0.0049 to 0.0788 after Holm and FDR rules. Likewise, gaps among the top three Census Public Use Microdata Areas (PUMAs) show p = 0.469 to 0.730 across 80 weights.

Three rules help counties use data well and focus on large losses. When data allow error checks, analysts use multi-test rules and 1,000 random-label tests. There, the max chance area under the curve (AUC) score is 0.593 versus 0.724 on real data. On monthly DHCS tables, a 100-member minimum refers small language groups to CBO partners. County call teams then rank 100+ groups by losses past the -12.77% county rate. That rule puts Spanish first (-24,880 net, -7,230 extra). Overlapping groups ages 19-44 come next: Hispanic adults (-17,380 or 28.83% of county loss, -6,344 extra) and men (-15,496, -5,697 extra).

Common Questions and Study Notes

Why do small language groups like Samoan (-34.04%) and French (-25.00%) rank first when sorted only by percentage decline?
In a group of 47 members (Samoan) or 56 members (French), a net loss of 14 to 16 people causes a 25% to 34% drop. The monthly counts do not show why those people left. Under the proposed rule, staff refer groups under 100 members to CBO partners. That way, big percent changes in small groups do not cut calls to groups with thousands of lost members.
Why can't monthly DHCS totals support a simple test of individual exits, and when do multiple-test rules apply?
Monthly DHCS tables show net totals (entries minus exits) and count the same person across age and language rows. Family members in one home also gain or lose coverage together. Because single exits are unseen, simple exit tests fail on monthly totals. When sampling error is measured, multi-test rules cut false positives in 80-weight survey tests (p = 0.469 to 0.730), 16-delay checks (p = 0.0788), and 1,000 label swaps (0.593 max chance score).
How does ranking by net enrollment losses beyond the county's 12.77% decline help set Medi-Cal call priorities?
This rule compares a group's actual net change with what a 12.77% drop predicts from its June 2025 count. Among 138,215 Spanish speakers, 12.77% predicts -17,650, so the -24,880 drop shows 7,230 extra lost members (-7,230). Hispanic adults 19-44 show -6,344 extra losses, and men 19-44 show -5,697 extra losses. Those counts show where to focus bilingual calls, though monthly totals do not show if calls changed coverage.
