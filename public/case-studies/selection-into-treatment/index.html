Higher Low-Wage Leave Pay and Small-Firm Job Rules Affect Who Uses Paid Family Leave

Modeling how 90% low-wage leave pay, five-worker job rules, and firm pay affect leave claims.

In practice today: All 18.5 million workers in California State Disability Insurance pay payroll tax on all wages, and SB 951 pays 90% of wages to lower earners.[1][2] Yet state Paid Family Leave pay ends at 8 weeks while job protection lasts 12 weeks at 5+ worker firms.[2]

What our model estimates: Suppose claimants use 35% of four added weeks on average across 369,600 claims (+39,600 added).[1][3] Gross SDI cost reaches $623.7M/yr (1.345% tax rate) and adds $422.0M in net worker pay, with a +$19.7M net gain for the lowest wage group.[2][3]

Policy option: State budget teams can plan SDI reserves on $623.7M gross cash rather than the $1,910.0M full-use limit.[3] Clinics can also add leave claim help to WIC and Medi-Cal prenatal visits.[2]

In the expected case, that 30% offset reduces added worker pay from $602.8M gross benefits to $422.0M net statewide ($410.9M summed across the four rounded group shares in Table 1). Expand scenario notes

• Case 1 Lower Use (25% of Added Weeks / +5% New Claims). Claims rise by +16,500 above the 330,000 baseline to 346,500 per year. Gross SDI spending is $417.8M/yr ($2.688B across 2025–2030, 1.330% tax rate), and net worker pay rises by $201.8M/yr.

• Case 2 Expected Use (35% of Added Weeks / +12% New Claims). Claims rise by +39,600 to 369,600 per year. Gross SDI spending is $623.7M/yr ($4.013B across 2025–2030, 1.345% tax rate), and net worker pay rises by $422.0M/yr.

• Case 3 Full-Use Upper Limit (100% of Added Weeks / +20% New Claims). Claims rise by +66,000 to 396,000 per year. Gross SDI spending reaches $1,910.0M/yr ($12.286B across 2025–2030). That cost leaves a 2026 SDI balance of -$0.58B, equal to -3.0% of projected 2026 SDI spending.

Why Worker Leave Choices Affect Both Fund Reserves and Tax Fairness

In program studies, selection into treatment means people who use a program differ from people who do not. In Paid Family Leave (PFL), who claims added weeks shapes both fund costs and tax fairness. Under the California Family Rights Act (CFRA), firms with five or more workers must hold jobs for 12 weeks. Yet state PFL pay ends after week 8. Because hourly families have less cash saved, many return to work early while salaried staff with employer leave pay take all 12 weeks.

Modeling leave use alongside payroll taxes avoids two budget errors. Setting reserves for 100% full use ($1,910.0M gross per year and a -$0.58B deficit) estimates three times the expected cost. By contrast, assuming longer leave helps mainly high earners misses how Senate Bill 951 changed payroll taxes. Because SB 951 raised low-wage pay to 90% and taxed all covered wages, expected 35% use ($623.7M gross at a 1.345% rate, $422.0M net worker pay) provides +$23.0M in net yearly gains to the bottom two wage groups (+$19.7M and +$3.3M).

Common Questions and Study Notes

Why does Paid Family Leave use differ across wage groups when every covered worker pays into the fund?
All 18.5 million SDI-covered workers pay payroll tax. Yet lower-paid hourly workers could not afford large pay cuts before 90% leave pay began. Workers at firms with fewer than five staff also lack CFRA job protection.
How did Senate Bill 951 change who pays and who benefits in California Paid Family Leave?
Senate Bill 951 ended the wage cap on January 1, 2024, so high earners now pay SDI tax on all covered wages. On January 1, 2025, the law raised weekly leave pay to 90% for lower-paid workers. Under a 12-week plan, those two rules give a modeled +$19.7M yearly net gain to the lowest wage group.
Why do Employment Development Department (EDD) forecasts separate $623.7M gross SDI cost from $422.0M net worker pay?
Many salaried workers get private paid leave from their employers. When firms coordinate pay (modeled at 30% in the expected case), state PFL checks replace part of employer pay instead of adding dollar for dollar ($422.0M net vs. $602.8M gross benefits). Even so, the state SDI fund must still pay the full $623.7M cash bill.
