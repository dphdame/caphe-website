County Health Spending Links Show Higher Value Than Cost Under Three Federal Rates If Causal

Testing death-risk dollar values for pre-2020 county links, if causal, next to clinic pay offsets.

In practice today: County budget boards often ask whether program value still exceeds cost under lower federal rates or wider uncertainty bounds.[1][2] Federal guidance sets three death-risk rates: $6.3M, $13.6M, and $20.7M.[1]

What the comparison shows: If pre-2020 county spending links caused fewer deaths, yearly ratios span 15.0:1 to 49.3:1 (5.4:1 unadjusted low case), and 13-year links (adj. p = 0.067, above 0.05) span 27.2:1 to 89.4:1.[1][2] Separately, care worker visit fees pay 6.5% to 9.8% of $103,719 worker cost, while plan and hospital savings reach $107,006 and $291,171.[3]

Policy option: Budget reports can show all three federal rates alongside p-values and state clearly that death-risk ratios are non-cash values if causal.[1][2] For care workers, health-plan contracts cover pay better than higher visit quotas.[3]

• How County Value per Dollar Is Calculated (Unit A). If $10 more in county public health spending per resident caused the estimated decrease in deaths, value per dollar equals (fewer deaths per 100,000 / 100,000) × federal value / $10. At 2.38 fewer deaths per 100,000 and $13.6M, this gives 0.0000238 × $13,600,000 / $10 = 32.4:1 ($420,168 per death avoided). Equal benefits and costs (1.0:1) would require 0.0735 fewer deaths per 100,000 at $13.6M.

• Why County Results and Worker Costs Stay Separate. The county cases show how changing the death estimate or federal dollar value changes resident health value if causal. The worker cases show how clinic fees and hospital savings compare with one $103,719 worker position by payer. Their dollar amounts are never added together.

Keeping Death-Risk Dollar Rates Apart From Causal Checks and Clinic Pay

Boards should check whether spending caused fewer deaths and how wide the 13-year range is. Across 2025 HHS values ($6.3M to $20.7M), all three rates exceed cost if spending caused fewer deaths. These figures are resident health values, not cash paid to counties. If causal, the -2.38 yearly link spans 15.0:1 to 49.3:1 (32.4:1 middle, or $420,168 per death). Under that rule, the -4.32 13-year link (adjusted p = 0.067, above 0.05) spans 27.2:1 to 89.4:1 (58.8:1 middle). Pairing unadjusted bounds with $6.3M gives 5.4:1 yearly and 8.2:1 over 13 years.

For care workers, changing who pays matters more than billing more clinic visits. Raising service units by 50%, from 213 to 320, moves paid worker costs from 6.5% ($6,775) to 9.8% ($10,162) of $103,719. By contrast, savings from fewer hospital stays reach 103.2% ($107,006) for Medi-Cal plans. Across all hospital payers, hospital stay savings reach 280.7% ($291,171). Thus, county estimates depend on causal proof and 13-year bounds (adjusted p = 0.067), while worker teams need plan contracts.

Common Questions and Study Notes

How do tests that change one or two inputs help explain these dollar estimates?
A one-way test changes one input, such as testing $6.3M, $13.6M, and $20.7M rates on the -2.38 yearly link (15.0:1 to 49.3:1 if causal). A two-input test pairs 0.85 from the unadjusted 95% range with $6.3M to give 5.4:1 per year if causal. Over 13 years, 8.2:1 uses the unadjusted -1.30 bound, while 4.32 has adjusted p = 0.067 (above 0.05). Showing all cases keeps causal rules and ranges clear.
How small could the decrease in deaths be before estimated benefits no longer exceed costs?
This check asks how many deaths $10 more per resident must prevent for value to equal cost (1.0:1) at federal rates. At the $13.6M middle HHS rate, equal value and cost need 0.0735 fewer deaths per 100,000 people. Pre-2020 links are 2.38 fewer deaths per 100,000 per year and 4.32 over 13 years (adjusted p = 0.067).
Why are care worker cost comparisons kept separate from death-risk dollar ratios?
County ratios estimate resident health value only if spending caused fewer deaths. They do not show cash paid to counties. Worker rows instead compare visit fees ($6,775-$10,162) and hospital stay savings ($107,006-$291,171) with one $103,719 worker. Keeping them apart shows worker teams need Medi-Cal plan contracts, not VSL talks.
