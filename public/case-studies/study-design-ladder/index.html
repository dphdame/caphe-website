Planning Outreach, Costing State Laws, and Checking Budgets Each Need a Matched Study Method

Matching linked records, trend checks, county data, and call trials to the question each method answers.

In practice today: Randomized trials can test outreach phone calls directly, yet counties cannot randomize a statewide law or 20 years of past budgets.[2][3][4] Using raw surveys or simple before-and-after counts introduces large starting errors.[1][2]

What the comparison shows: Linking state Medi-Cal rolls (255,601 adults) with Census shares fixes a 40.2% survey undercount and finds 120,239 work-rule adults.[1] Meanwhile, an 82,695-member renewal trial shows live calls add +8% to +11% renewals, while pre-2020 county records link $10/person to 2.38 yearly and 4.32 13-year fewer deaths (adj. p = 0.067).[3][4]

Policy option: Agencies can match each budget question to the method built for its data.[1][2] Linked rolls set bilingual staffing, baseline forecasts price state laws, and randomized call trials test daily outreach.[1][2][4]

Why Each Health Budget Question Needs a Matched Study Method

Ranking study methods in general misses a clear rule: the right method depends on the question. Planning Medi-Cal outreach links records to fix the 40.2% ACS undercount (152,811 survey adults vs. 255,601 on DHCS rolls). That step finds 120,239 work-rule adults, including 26,515 with limited English skills. Costing statewide PFL law SB 951 splits regular growth (325,055 to 330,000 claims) from +39,600 modeled claims. Checking county budgets tests COVID-19 aid (+2.47) apart from regular budgets. Regular spending links with 2.38 yearly and 4.32 13-year fewer deaths per 100,000 (adj. p = 0.067). Federal Value of a Statistical Life (VSL) benchmarks imply 32:1 and 59:1. Those ratios apply only if spending caused fewer deaths and are not cash returns.

Once member counts and budgets are set, trials and step checks guide daily work. In California's 82,695-member renewal trial, four text and email groups showed 0% renewal gain. Adding live phone calls raised Medi-Cal renewals by 8% to 11%. Splitting a 15.0% CHW rate into outreach started (27.8%), patients reached (69.2%), and care link (78.1%) also shows which steps patients miss. Field workers link nearly four-fifths of reached patients, so clinics should improve how outreach starts first.

Common Questions and Study Notes

Why does the Census ACS undercount Santa Clara County adult Medi-Cal members by 40.2%?
Survey respondents often report local managed-care plan names as private insurance or leave out other adults in the home. As a result, ACS counts 152,811 adult members versus 255,601 certified adults in DHCS records (-40.2%). Using DHCS records for the total and applying ACS shares estimates language needs and work hours without undercounting members.
What did California's 82,695-member renewal trial show about text messages versus live phone calls?
Across four random groups that varied text and email wording, the study found no increase in Medi-Cal renewals (0%). Only the fifth group, which added live phone navigator calls, raised renewals by 8% to 11% compared with the group without live calls (+1.0 to +1.8 percentage points). Live help resolves problems completing forms that text wording alone cannot fix.
How does checking outreach started (27.8%), contact (69.2%), and care connection (78.1%) help clinics?
Looking only at the 15.0% overall rate (0.278 × 0.692 × 0.781) makes CHW outreach appear weak. Checking the three referral steps one by one shows that CHWs connect 78.1% of contacted patients with care. Because the largest loss occurs before clinics start outreach (27.8%), clinics should improve the steps used to start outreach first.
