Checking Telehealth With Other Medicaid Care Shows Distinct Growth Rates as More Adults Join

Checking telehealth biller counts with other care codes and pre-2020 trends over 84 months.

Checking telehealth codes next to other Medicaid codes puts biller growth in context. From 2020 to 2023, federal rules paused renewals and kept millions more adults enrolled. State offices, such as the California Department of Health Care Services (DHCS), cannot judge telehealth trends from telehealth-eligible counts alone. Subtracting growth in other codes gives a descriptive difference, not a proven policy gain. Only one pre-2020 year exists, and clinics could bill visits under either code group.

Checking telehealth-eligible codes with other Medicaid care clarifies growth across 84 months of U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) records.[1] From 2019 to 2023, monthly billers on telehealth codes rose +24.33%, from 91,399 to 113,638.[1] Over those same years, billers on other Medicaid codes rose +18.71%, from 109,087 to 129,492.[1] Subtracting that 18.71% growth leaves a recorded difference of +5.62 percentage points (+5.62 pp) through 2023.[1] Through 2024, the recorded difference is +4.60 pp full-year, or +5.26 pp through October.[1] Yet from 2018 to 2019, telehealth counts already grew +1.21 pp faster (84,422 vs. 101,905 in 2018).[2] Extending that one-year difference puts the 2023 range at +0.78 pp to +5.62 pp (descriptive, not causal).[1][2] Both counts fell in 2024, with a -0.37 pp larger drop on telehealth codes.[1]

• Descriptive Growth Differences and Limits on Causal Claims. From 2019 to 2023, monthly billers on telehealth-eligible codes grew +24.33% (91,399 to 113,638), and other Medicaid codes grew +18.71% (109,087 to 129,492). Subtracting the two gives 24.33% − 18.71% = +5.62 pp (11.57% − 6.97% = +4.60 pp through 2024; -10.26% − [-9.89%] = -0.37 pp in 2023–2024; and 8.26% − 7.05% = +1.21 pp in 2018–2019). Providers can bill both code groups or change which codes they use. Pandemic rules also affected visit types differently, and only one pre-policy year is available. These differences therefore describe the records and do not prove a policy effect.

• Late-2024 Decline Across Both Groups. Both provider counts fell sharply in November and December 2024 (telehealth-eligible: 88,364 and 47,562; other codes: 101,476 and 57,878). National totals alone do not prove the cause, though late year-end claim reporting and resumed eligibility checks are possible reasons. Checking January–October 2024 separately (108,781 telehealth-eligible, +19.02% vs. 2019; 124,092 other codes, +13.76%) gives a +5.26 pp descriptive difference.

How Two-Group Checks Show Growth Differences Without Claiming Policy Cause

Checking one code group alone mixes wider coverage growth into telehealth counts. Federal rules paused Medicaid renewals from 2020 to 2023 and restarted them in 2023 and 2024. Monthly billers on telehealth codes rose +24.33% (91,399 to 113,638) from 2019 to 2023. Over the same years, billers on other Medicaid codes rose +18.71% (109,087 to 129,492).

Subtracting growth in other codes over the same dates leaves a +5.62 percentage-point difference through 2023. Through 2024, the recorded difference is +4.60 pp. From 2023 to 2024, the drop was 0.37 percentage points larger on telehealth codes (-0.37 pp). Telehealth billers already grew +1.21 pp faster in 2018-2019 (+8.26% vs. +7.05%). Extending that prior gap lowers the 2023 figure to +0.78 pp. Still, two code groups and one pre-policy year cannot rule out visit changes across codes. Reporting +0.78 pp to +5.62 pp as a descriptive range keeps claims within what these records show.

Common Questions and Study Notes

What does subtracting growth in other Medicaid codes show, and what are its limits?
Comparing telehealth-eligible codes (+24.33%) with other Medicaid codes (+18.71%) from 2019 to 2023 leaves a +5.62 percentage-point recorded difference. Most of the raw increase happened alongside wider Medicaid growth. Still, this difference is descriptive, not causal. Clinics can bill both code groups, and pandemic rules affected visit types differently.
Why did both telehealth-eligible (-10.26%) and other Medicaid billers (-9.89%) fall at nearly the same rate in 2024?
Both provider counts fell sharply in late 2024, with a -0.37 percentage-point difference between them. National provider counts alone do not prove the cause, though resumed eligibility renewals and late year-end claim reporting are possible reasons. The similar drops show that the decline affected both code groups rather than telehealth alone.
What would need to be true to say the policy caused the growth difference?
Without new telehealth rules, both code groups would need to have grown at the same percentage rate after 2019. Providers would also need not to move visits between code groups. Because only one pre-2020 year exists (+1.21 pp faster on telehealth codes), +0.78 pp to +5.62 pp is a descriptive range, not a causal effect.
