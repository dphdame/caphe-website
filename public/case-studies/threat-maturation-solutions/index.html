Multi-Year Fund Models Separate Normal Payroll Growth From Paid-Leave Law Costs

Separating routine job and wage growth from modeled 12-week Paid Family Leave costs through 2030.

Statewide Paid Family Leave (PFL) laws start on one date for every covered worker. That single start date leaves no comparison counties in California. At the same time, job growth, rising wages, and payroll taxes on all wages (with no cap) raise fund totals each year. Analysts call that normal baseline growth maturation. Because payrolls grow each year, a year-to-year subtraction mixes normal payroll growth into the cost of adding leave weeks.

Splitting normal growth from added leave weeks clarifies California State Disability Insurance (SDI) reserves.[1][3] Without a 12-week law, normal growth raises PFL claims from 325,055 in 2025 to 330,000 in 2026.[1] Normal growth also adds +$1.635B in 2026 SDI spending and raises reserves from $3.19B (18.2% of spending) to $10.29B (50.7%) by 2030.[1][3] Above that baseline, a 12-week plan at 35% added-week use adds +39,600 claims (369,600 total) and +$623.7M/yr.[2] That cost lowers 2026 reserves to $1.96B (10.8%), or $2.36B (13.2%) at 25% use vs. -$0.58B at 100% use.[3] Taxes on all wages then raise reserves to $6.37B (30.4%) by 2030.[2][3] That level is above the Employment Development Department (EDD) 25.0% reserve goal (not a legal minimum).[3]

• Separate Start Dates Under SB 951. Senate Bill 951 took effect on two separate dates. First, the law ended the SDI taxable wage cap on January 1, 2024, taxing 100% of covered wages ($1,371.5B in 2026). Second, the law raised weekly leave pay to 90% of wages for lower-paid workers on January 1, 2025.

• Types of Paid Family Leave Claims. Across the EDD Paid Family Leave Monthly Series (2025 actuals: 325,055 first claims paid), newborn and adoption bonding makes up 87.8% of claims (285,408). Family caregiving makes up 12.2% (39,624), and military family needs make up <0.1% (23).

Why Fund Forecasts Must Check Both First-Year Costs and Multi-Year Changes

Statewide laws like Senate Bill 951 take effect on one date for all 18.5 million covered workers. That statewide start leaves no comparison counties inside California. Employment Development Department (EDD) forecasts therefore separate law changes from routine job, wage, and claim growth. Even without a new law, +1.52% claim growth (325,055 to 330,000) and +6.88% check growth ($1,090 to $1,165) add +$1.635B to 2026 State Disability Insurance (SDI) spending.

Checking both Year-1 costs and 2026-2030 tax revenue changes clarifies reserve planning. Looking at 2026 alone, a 12-week Paid Family Leave plan (+39,600 claims to 369,600; +$623.7M/yr) lowers reserves from $3.19B (18.2%) to $1.96B (10.8%). Yet payroll taxes on all covered wages grow faster than weekly checks capped at $1,765. By 2030, baseline reserves reach $10.29B (50.7%), and the 12-week plan rises to $6.37B (30.4%). That 30.4% balance exceeds EDD's 25.0% reserve planning goal (not a legal minimum), while payroll tax rates up to the 1.5% cap follow Cal. Unemp. Ins. Code § 984.

Common Questions and Study Notes

How does a multi-year fund model improve on a one-year before-and-after subtraction?
A one-year subtraction mixes routine job and wage growth (+$1.635B in 2026 SDI spending) with added leave benefits. By contrast, a multi-year model projects routine growth first. That split separates the first-year policy cost (+$623.7M) from later yearly reserve growth.
Why does California's baseline SDI reserve rise from 18.2% ($3.19B) in 2026 to 50.7% ($10.29B) by 2030?
Senate Bill 951 ended the wage cap on worker SDI taxes on January 1, 2024, taxing all $1,371.5B of covered wages. Weekly benefit checks, however, remain capped ($1,765 per week in 2026). Because high wages are taxed with no cap while checks are capped, tax revenue grows faster than benefit payments.
How do SDI reserves change from 2026 to 2030 under the expected 12-week leave plan?
In 2026, adding +39,600 claims (+$623.7M gross cost) lowers reserves from $3.19B (18.2%) to $1.96B (10.8%). That balance is below EDD's 25.0% ($4.38B) planning goal (not a legal minimum), while payroll tax authority up to 1.5% comes from Cal. Unemp. Ins. Code § 984. Payroll taxes on all covered wages then increase reserves each year to $6.37B (30.4%) by 2030.
