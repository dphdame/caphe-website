Changes in Data Counting Rules Change Food-Access Scores Even When Store Counts Stay the Same

Comparing map searches, SNAP store lists, county health permits, and five-year surveys across 408 tracts.

County food reports combine store lists from map companies (via an application programming interface, or API) with health permits. They also use Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) store lists. Five-year American Community Survey (ACS) tables add data across census tracts, which are small Census areas. Each source counts food stores or homes receiving food aid by its own rule. Map lists include cafes, permit logs list deli counters apart, and five-year surveys average earlier years. When a counting rule changes, numbers shift even when no stores open or close.

Changing counting rules changes food-access numbers with zero change in actual stores.[1][2] Moving from 6,613 map food listings to 514 SNAP Electronic Benefit Transfer (EBT) stores nearly doubles mean distance.[1] Trips rise from 0.29 to 0.573 miles.[1] Even so, 81.7% of top-need tracts stay in the highest fifth, and 65.2% of all 408 tracts keep the same fifth.[1][2] In county Department of Environmental Health (DEH) files, combining 1,220 duplicate counter records (14.3% of 8,555) leaves 7,335 sites.[2] That step finds 755 grocers (including 724 confirmed grocers) at 97.5% accuracy in a 489-site check.[2] Pairing five-year ACS maps with monthly California Department of Social Services (CDSS) rolls also shows recent changes.[3]

Why Staff Must Check Counting Rules Before Reading Report Changes

Analysts often combine map lists, SNAP lists, permit logs, and surveys without checking their rules. Yet each source defines a food store or enrolled home in its own way. Replacing one old map search group with several groups raises listings from 344 to 6,613. Using 514 SNAP EBT stores nearly doubles mean distance, from 0.29 to 0.573 miles. Raw DEH permit files also list deli counters apart, adding 1,220 extra rows across 8,555 permits (14.3%). Meanwhile, five-year ACS averages make sudden enrollment rises look smaller, showing -4.94% in 2020 while monthly CDSS rolls rose 56% to 71%.

Checking sudden report changes against agency records before changing grants prevents funding errors. Combining Santa Clara DEH permits by street address leaves 7,335 sites and 755 classified (724 confirmed) grocers at 97.5% accuracy. Across all 408 tracts, 65.2% stay in the same fifth after switching to EBT stores. Also, 81.7% of top-need tracts stay in the highest fifth. Using confirmed SNAP EBT lists and pairing ACS maps with monthly CDSS rolls keeps funding tied to checked data.

Common Questions and Study Notes

Why did 81.7% of top-need tracts stay in the top fifth when EBT checks nearly doubled average distance from 0.29 to 0.573 miles?
In the five-part food-need score, poverty (30%) and SNAP use (25%) account for 55% of the score, while store distance accounts for 20%. Removing cafes and snack shops increased measured distance in higher-income and lower-income tracts alike. As a result, 65.2% of all 408 tracts stayed in the same fifth and 81.7% of top-fifth tracts (67 of 82) remained in that fifth.
Why did the 2020 five-year ACS show a 4.94% SNAP decline while state CalFresh enrollment increased by 56% to 71%?
Each five-year ACS release combines survey responses collected over 60 months, so four-fifths of the 2020 file came from 2016-2019 before COVID-19. Survey collection in 2020 also had lower response rates among low-income renters. By contrast, monthly CDSS state records counted new CalFresh sign-ups right away.
Why must county health permits be combined by street address before counting grocery stores?
Santa Clara DEH files hold 8,555 active records because one supermarket often holds separate permits for its main floor, bakery, deli, and meat counter. Combining those 1,220 duplicate records (14.3% of raw records) by address leaves 7,335 distinct sites and 755 classified (724 confirmed) grocers at 97.5% accuracy. That check shows the map list had 6,613 food businesses, 8.76 times the 755 grocers from permit records, because it included non-grocery shops.
