State & Local Program Evaluation

Three Questions in Public Health Resource Allocation

Health economics evaluates public health programs by measuring net expenditures, counterfactual health outcomes, and provider incentives.

1. Health Returns

What health outcomes do public health investments produce?

Estimating mortality and hospitalization reductions using empirical control strategies to estimate whether changes resulted from the intervention rather than secular background trends.

Lewbel IV Model Estimate Each $10/capita in local public health spending is estimated to reduce county mortality by 9.16 deaths per 100,000 (Methodology).

2. Access & Incentives

Why do programs succeed or stall despite state funding increases?

Analyzing how provider reimbursement rates, administrative burden, and patient cost-sharing shape participation and service delivery across California communities.

California Administrative Data (NPPES + Claims) Medi-Cal billing participation varies widely by county and specialty, highlighting possible localized access gaps.
Explore Medi-Cal Provider Access Explorer →
3. Resource Allocation

How should finite funds be allocated between competing programs?

Comparing cost per outcome, budget feasibility, and opportunity cost when choosing between upstream prevention and clinical treatment programs under fixed budgets.

Analytical Framework Formal cost-effectiveness (ICERs) and budget-impact analysis to support transparent policy choices.

Analytical Framework

How Economics Evaluates Public Health

Health economics evaluates disease outcomes while accounting for behavioral drivers, institutional incentives, and finite resources.

1. Counterfactual Analysis

Track 1 Tutorials →

Estimating what disease incidence, utilization, and costs would have been in the absence of a program, using empirical control strategies rather than simple before-and-after observations.

2. Incentives & Behavioral Drivers

Track 2 Tutorials →

Analyzing how provider reimbursement, patient cost-sharing, and organizational constraints drive program uptake and health outcomes across California communities.

3. Resource Constraints & Scale

Track 3 Tutorials →

Evaluating fixed versus variable costs, workforce capacity, and budget impact when scaling pilot interventions to jurisdiction-wide or statewide programs.

About CAPHE

Supporting Economic Analysis in California Public Health

CAPHE is a professional association for economists, evaluators, and analysts working in California public health. Through monthly peer review, interactive methods labs, and working groups, we support practical economic analysis across state agencies and local health departments.
