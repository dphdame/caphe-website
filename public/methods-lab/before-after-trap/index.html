Pre-Existing Workforce Trends Account for a Large Share of Projected Medicaid Telehealth Growth

Comparing multi-year provider trends with modeled post-policy jumps across Medicaid claims.

When auditing a telehealth rollout by comparing billing providers in 2019 against 2023, a two-year check mixes new pay rules with workforce growth that was already underway. Across 84 months of Medicaid claims (2018 to 2024), monthly billing providers for telehealth-eligible services grew from 91,399 in 2019 to 113,638 in 2023 (+22,239 providers, or +24.33%).[1] A modeled two-point check overstates the policy jump. That provider list was already adding 6,977 billers per year (+8.26%) before 2020.[2] Checking multi-year base slopes next to other care (+18.71%) keeps budget teams from over-funding routine trend growth.[3]

Mean monthly billing providers rose from 91,399 in 2019 to 113,638 in 2023. A two-point check assigns all +22,239 new billers to post-2019 rules. Yet the count was already rising from 84,422 in 2018. Measured through 2024, the raw gain shrinks to +10,579 (+11.57%) after a -11,660 (-10.26%) drop in 2024. Ruler 2: Mode A Base-Trend Gap: -5,669 vs. Base Path (2023). The straight-line path extends the 2018 to 2019 pace: 91,399 + 4 × 6,977 = 119,307 in 2023. The actual count of 113,638 sits 5,669 below that line (-4.75%). By 2024 the gap reaches -24,306 (126,284 projected vs. 101,978 actual). A compound +8.26% annual rate gives -11,911 in 2023. Both paths show how much the result rests on the chosen base trend.

How Multi-Year Trends Improve on Two-Point Checks

Yearly program reviews that compare one pre-policy year against the latest year mix prior workforce growth with the policy change. A simple two-point gap cannot tell whether a rule caused a jump. A multi-year trend may have kept climbing on its old slope, or a broad enrollment wave may have lifted every service line at once.

Checking the full 2018 to 2024 series sharpens budget review. Billing providers for video care were already growing +8.26% per year before 2020. Other Medicaid care grew +18.71% from 2019 to 2023 next to the +24.33% video care gain. A straight-line path from 2018 to 2019 leaves 2023 at 5,669 providers below trend, and the compound path leaves it 11,911 below. Multi-year base trends thus work best as a check on two-point claims.

Mode Classification (Mode A on Observed Series): Billing provider counts, code counts, claims, paid dollars, and member-months come from the HHS Medicaid Provider Spending file (telehealth_month_summary.parquet, Jan 2018 to Dec 2024). The unit of analysis is care-group-month (2 groups × 84 months = 168 rows). The straight-line and compound paths are Mode A trend checks on these observed series.

Primary Data Sources: U.S. HHS Medicaid Provider Spending File, Medicaid.gov Open Data, CMS T-MSIS Analytic Files Documentation, and California DHCS Telehealth Policy Guidance.

Common Questions & Method Notes

What is a raw pre-post check in health program review?
A raw pre-post check subtracts one pre-policy year from one post-policy year. It credits the whole gap to the new rule and leaves out ongoing base trends and broad system shifts.
How does the Medicaid series show the limits of a two-point check?
Monthly billing providers for telehealth-eligible care rose 22,239 (+24.33%) from 2019 to 2023. Yet the count had already grown 6,977 (+8.26%) from 2018 to 2019. Other Medicaid care also rose 18.71% from 2019 to 2023.
What is the first data check to run before using a pre-post number?
Plot at least 12 to 24 months of pre-policy data to see if the count was already rising or falling. Then compare that path against a service line that did not get the policy change.
