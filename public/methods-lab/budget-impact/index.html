Multi-Year Return Models Require Year-One Cash-Flow and Reserve Schedules for Budget Approval

Pairing modeled long-run returns with yearly cash outlays, reserve floors, and budget line rules.

Hiring full-time Community Health Workers or expanding leave benefits creates a Year 1 cash gap when pay bills arrive before multi-year care savings. In California, county prevention spending yields a 125:1 multi-year return and a modeled 52:1 return in Year 1, while Paid Family Leave expansion brings a projected $422.0M per year in net worker pay.[1] Long-run return ratios omit single-year cash rules because leave expansion needs $623.7M in projected 2026 cash, dipping the state reserve to 10.8% ($1.96B) against a 25.0% ($4.38B) goal.[2] Pairing multi-year return models with a 6-year cash schedule (+0.045 rate points) gives budget staff a full funding plan.[3]

Why Analysts Pair Return Ratios with a Multi-Year Cash Table

A frequent gap between long-run value models and yearly county budgets happens when an analyst shows a high social return (such as 125:1 for county health spending or $422M in net worker pay for Paid Family Leave) without a Year 1 cash and reserve schedule. Budget staff and fund actuaries do not doubt the social value. They pause because they must balance a 1-year cash ledger, hold reserve floors (such as the 25% EDD goal), and follow strict line-item rules.

Showing both rulers side by side turns a budget dispute into a clear funding plan. Analysts can show how much gross cash is needed in Year 1 ($623.7M for PFL, or $150K-$600K for local renewal aid), when reserves dip (2026 at 10.8%) and rebuild (2030 at 30.4%), and which group holds the cash savings. Leaders can then set the exact payroll rate lift (+0.045 points) or plan transfer needed to pay the bill.

Mode Classification (Mode B Enacted Budgets & Slopes + Mode A Cash-Flow & Lag Models): Enacted FY 2026-27 state Medi-Cal budget lines ($709M total / $196.8M GF, $3.3B county admin, ended $20M AB 102 grants), 2025 SDI actuals ($3.011B balance, 18.9% ratio), and the 5-year LHA slope (-9.16, -4.56 base split) are Mode B facts. Multi-year 2026-2030 SDI reserve paths, county renewal tool budgets ($150K-$600K per year), and the 5-year phase-in schedule weights (0.42, 0.67, 0.82, 0.91, 1.00) are Mode A projections.

Common Questions & Method Notes

What is the difference between Cost-Effectiveness Analysis (CEA) and Budget Impact Analysis (BIA)?
CEA and return ratios measure long-run value per dollar across all groups. Budget Impact Analysis (BIA) tracks yearly cash inflows, cash bills, reserve floors, and line-item rules for one payer over 1 to 5 years.
Why must the state SDI fund budget $623.7M per year when net worker gains are $422.0M per year?
A 30% private firm offset trims the net new pay gain for workers ($422.0M vs. $602.8M gross claims) when firms reduce their own top-off pay. However, the state SDI fund must still pay the full $602.8M in claims plus $21.1M in admin ($623.7M total).
How fast do the death reductions from county public health spending arrive?
Under the 5-year phase-in schedule in the 57-county CAPHE LHA model, 42% of the drop (3.85 fewer deaths per 100,000, a 52:1 return) arrives in Year 1. It rises to 67% in Year 2, 82% in Year 3 (102:1 return), 91% in Year 4, and 100% (9.16 fewer deaths per 100,000, 125:1 return) in Year 5.
