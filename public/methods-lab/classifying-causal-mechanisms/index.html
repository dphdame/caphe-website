Post-Enrollment Clinic-Visit Controls Remove the Primary Care Pathway From Modeled Hospital Evaluations

Splitting pre-program background factors from care steps on the path in modeled hospital savings checks.

When an analyst models how food access links to diabetes while holding high blood pressure fixed, the regression drops part of the diet-to-disease path, while adjusting for baseline poverty isolates the income gap. In California Community Health Worker evaluations, leaving post-start clinic visits open keeps the full 30% drop in hospital stays tied to a 14.6 point rise in primary care visits (71.7% vs. 57.1%).[1] Controlling for post-start clinic visits removes that care path, while restricting a provider test set to 55,018 active 2024 billers leaves only 9 of 95 excluded providers (after 90.5% exited before 2024).[2] Adjusting only for pre-program traits keeps the full policy impact intact.[3]

• Program Step Over-Control Math: Suppose treatment X shifts care step M (M = α·X + u) and Y = β_direct·X + γ·M + ε. The total effect of X on Y is τ_total = β_direct + α·γ. Adding M as a control estimates only β_direct. The share removed is α·γ / τ_total. It reaches 100% only when β_direct = 0. The CAPHE CHW inputs (71.7% vs. 57.1%, OR = 1.90; -30% hospital stays) do not split those two pieces.

• Separate Datasets (Mode A Synthesis): The four columns show roles using four separate CAPHE files. Values are observed base inputs from each file. They are not from one joint regression and should not be compared across columns.

• Subgroup Selection Note: In the linked Medicaid file, the 55,018 providers still billing in 2024 (9 exclusions vs. 55,009 controls) show an observed screening AUC of 0.442 (95% CI 0.212-0.664), compared with 0.724 (95% CI 0.667-0.777) across all 487,479 providers and 0.653 when exit-year reweighted. Months Active has a SHAP correlation of r = -0.9009, and excluded providers sit at the 38.0th vs. 50.0th median peer percentile of active months, though with 9 active cases the subgroup difference does not isolate early exit as the sole cause.

• Instrument & Unit Note: Instruments play a different role from controls. In the LHA panel (57 California counties, 2003-2023), county budgets react to death rates. The 9.16 fewer deaths/100K IV estimate uses Lewbel (2012) instruments (first-stage F = 314.1, Hansen J = 4.49, p = 0.343) in Stage 1 of 2SLS instead of as outcome controls. Across source files, Tables 1-5 of lewbel_iv_results_section.tex label -9.158 per $1/capita, while Section 4.4 and lha-calculator.js standardize -9.16 per $10/capita (-0.916 per $1/capita).

Why Mapping the System Path Comes Before Running the Model

Adding every health and income variable to a tract regression mixes pre-program income gaps with health steps that sit on the path between food and chronic disease. If a variable is a pre-program confounder (such as poverty explaining 62.2% of tract food-need variance), leaving it out slants the slope. If a variable is a post-start care step, such as a Community Health Worker linking a patient to primary care (raising visits from 57.1% to 71.7%, OR = 1.90) to prevent a hospital stay, controlling for clinic visits subtracts part of the program's gain.

Before running a model, health economists map each variable in a causal diagram. Confounders are shared causes measured before treatment, so analysts control for them. Care steps sit on the causal path, so analysts leave them open when measuring total impact. Colliders and post-outcome filters like active 2024 billing alter the evaluation sample (where the 2024 active subset shows an observed 0.442 AUC vs. 0.724 on the full list), so analysts avoid filtering on them. Instruments shift treatment with no direct link to the outcome, so analysts use them in Stage 1 (as the LHA panel does with F = 314.1).

Mode Classification (Mode A Synthesis on Observed Inputs): The role rules and model guide are a Mode A synthesis. The numbers in Table 1 (tract poverty R² = 62.2%; CHW primary care visits 71.7% vs. 57.1% TOT, OR = 1.90, and -30% hospital stays; Medicaid screening AUC = 0.724 full list vs. observed AUC = 0.442 in the 2024 active subset; LHA first-stage F = 314.1, J = 4.49, p = 0.343) are observed outputs from four separate CAPHE files.

Common Questions & Method Notes

Why shouldn't an analyst control for post-start clinic visits when measuring whether CHWs cut hospital stays?
Raising primary care visits (from 57.1% to 71.7% TOT, OR = 1.90) is a main care step through which CHWs prevent hospital stays (-30%). Controlling for post-start clinic visits holds that step fixed. That blocks part of the program path and understates total CHW value.
How can an analyst tell whether a variable is a confounder or a care step?
Check the clock and the direction of cause. A confounder is set BEFORE the program starts (like baseline age, pre-program poverty, or county land area) and affects both participation and health. A care step changes AFTER the program starts because of the program.
What is an instrument, and why is it kept out of the outcome controls?
An instrument shifts the policy input (here, county public health spending) without a direct path to the health outcome. It enters Stage 1 of two-stage least squares to isolate spending shifts that are not caused by death spikes. In the LHA panel of 57 California counties (2003-2023), the instruments have a first-stage F of 314.1 and Hansen J of 4.49 (p = 0.343). Adding an instrument as an outcome control would absorb the clean spending variation.
