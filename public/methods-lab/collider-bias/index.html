Active-Only Provider Subsets Omit Earlier-Exiting Sanctioned Billers in Modeled Risk Audits

Comparing modeled full-list provider risk screens against active-biller subsets that drop providers who left early.

When an audit team tests a Medicaid billing risk model only on providers still active in the latest year, the check drops sanctioned billers who already left the program. Across 487,479 Medicaid billing providers, the screening model flags sanctioned providers at 9.5 times the random rate in the top 1% of alerts (9 caught cases across 4,844 flags).[1] In the subset of 55,018 providers still billing in 2024 (9 exclusions), the screen falls below a coin-flip rank alongside shorter prior tenure (38.0th vs. 50.0th percentile), an observed subgroup difference instead of an isolated causal effect.[2] Calibrated alert rates across the full provider list set realistic caseload goals for audit staff.[3]

• Target Definition: The positive class is a formal OIG LEIE exclusion under Specification A financial fraud statutes (1128(a)(1), 1128(a)(3), 1128(b)(1)). The prospective check scores the 95 providers excluded in 2025 or later against 487,384 non-excluded controls (487,479 total).

• Bayes Base-Rate Rescaling (Mode A Calibration): The holdout keeps all 95 cases and 97,476 of 487,384 controls (20%). False positives scale by 1 / 0.20 = 5. In odds form, Odds_pop = Odds_holdout / 5. For Top 1%, 0.922% / 99.078% = 0.009306, and dividing by 5 gives 0.001861, or 0.186% precision. For Top 5%, 0.512% rescales to 0.103%. Lift equals precision divided by base rate: 0.1858% / 0.0195% = 9.53x, and 0.1029% / 0.0195% = 5.28x.

• Observed Subgroup Difference & Exit-Timing Sensitivity: In the empirical data, 90.5% of post-panel fraud providers stopped billing prior to 2024 (vs. 43.6% of controls), and excluded providers sit at the 38.0th vs. 50.0th median peer percentile of active months (Months Active SHAP correlation r = -0.9009). Reweighting controls to match the fraud exit-year distribution shifts prospective AUC from 0.724 to 0.653, and restricting to the 55,018 providers still billing in 2024 (9 exclusions vs. 55,009 controls) yields an observed AUC of 0.442 (95% CI 0.212-0.664). These descriptive subgroup and reweighting comparisons document how conditioning on active 2024 billing alters the evaluation sample, but do not separately isolate early exit as the sole cause of the 0.442 subgroup score.

• Caseload & Cost: MFCU unit cost is $23,719 ($395.8M / 16,688 active cases, HHS-OIG FY 2024). Top 1% gives 4,844 × $23,719 = $114.9M and 4,844 / 16,688 = 29.0% of caseload. Top 5% gives 24,295 / 16,688 = 145.6%.

• Data Provenance: Values come from Mcaid/05_fraud_detection/paper/output/results.json (prospective_auc, active_subset_auc, top1_prec_holdout, top1_prec_pop, top1_flags, top1_captured, top1_lift, top5_*, mfcu_*, shap_months_active_r, matched_sample_rf_auc, entropy_balanced_rf_auc), Mcaid/05_fraud_detection/paper/main.tex (L398-402: 90.5% vs. 43.6% pre-2024 exit, 0.653 exit-year reweighted AUC), output/tables/tab6_economics.tex (N = 487,479, 0.0195%), and output/tables/tab_a3_base_rates.tex (peer percentiles).

Why Limiting a File to "Providers Still Billing" Shifts Every Ratio

Collider bias was first noted by Joseph Berkson in 1946 in hospital data. A related sample-selection shift appears in health claims when a team keeps only providers still billing in the latest year. In the linked Medicaid file, 90.5% of post-panel excluded billers stopped billing before 2024 (vs. 43.6% of controls), and reweighting controls by exit year shifts AUC from 0.724 to 0.653. In the subset of 55,018 providers still billing in 2024 (9 exclusions), the observed AUC is 0.442 (95% CI 0.212 to 0.664), reflecting both sample selection and the small number of remaining cases.

Downsampling shifts precision in a similar way. A holdout that keeps all 95 cases and 20% of controls reports 0.922% Top 1% precision. At the full-list base rate of 0.0195%, that same cutoff gives 0.186% precision, or 9 caught cases among 4,844 flags. Rescaling every subset score to the full billing list and checking the 16,688-case national MFCU caseload keeps agencies from sizing audit budgets on a filtered sample.

Mode Classification (Mode A Calibration on Observed Screening Results): AUC scores (0.724 full list, 0.653 exit-year reweighted, and 0.442 active-only subgroup), holdout precision, flags, caught cases, lift, and peer percentiles are observed outputs in output/results.json, main.tex (L398-402), output/tables/tab6_economics.tex, and output/tables/tab_a3_base_rates.tex. Rescaling from holdout to full-list precision is a Mode A calibration using Bayes rule. It matches the full-population precision in output/results.json (0.1858%).

Common Questions & Method Notes

What is a "collider" in a causal diagram, and how does it differ from a confounder?
A confounder is a shared CAUSE of both the treatment and the outcome (A <- C -> Y). Analysts control for it to remove bias. A collider is a shared EFFECT of two variables (A -> C <- Y). Filtering or controlling on a collider opens a false link between its causes.
How does the active-only subgroup score compare with the full-list score?
Across all 487,479 providers, the screen scores 0.724 AUC (95% CI 0.667 to 0.777). Because 90.5% of post-panel excluded providers stopped billing before 2024 (38.0th vs. 50.0th active-months percentile), reweighting controls by exit year shifts AUC to 0.653, and the 2024 active-biller subset (9 exclusions vs. 55,009 controls) shows an observed AUC of 0.442 (95% CI 0.212 to 0.664). That subgroup difference reflects sample restriction and the small 9-case sample instead of isolating a single cause.
How should analysts report precision from a downsampled holdout?
Rescale it to the full-list base rate. The holdout here keeps every case and 20% of controls. Top 1% precision of 0.922% rescales to 0.186% across the full billing list (0.0195% base rate), or 9 caught cases among 4,844 flags.
