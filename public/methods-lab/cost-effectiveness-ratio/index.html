Multi-Year Cost-Effectiveness Models Spread Year-One Startup Costs Across Ongoing Health Gains

Scoring modeled county care teams across startup and ongoing years using cost per healthy year and net value.

Judging a multi-year care team on Year 1 unit costs alone overstates the ongoing price of services that pay one-time training bills before steady health gains arrive. Across Santa Clara County's modeled 5-year, 750-client-per-year CHW program, direct operations produce 200.20 healthy life-years at $36,089 per healthy year and create +$12.80M in net value at the standard $100,000 benchmark.[1] Judging the team on Year 1 alone raises the unit cost to $48,202 per healthy year because $325,000 in startup costs lands before ongoing costs drop to $35,090.[2] Even with full startup included ($7,550,000 total, or $37,712 per healthy year), the 5-year model clears the strict $50,000 benchmark (+$2.46M net value).[3]

Why Net Dollar Value Pairs with the Cost Ratio in County Budgets

When analysts report a cost ratio ($36,089 per QALY operating, or $37,712 per QALY with startup), two practical questions arise from county leaders. First, how do we know whether $36,089 to $37,712 per QALY is a good value? Second, how do we compare a small team with a low ratio against a larger program with a slightly higher ratio? Value benchmarks ($50,000, $100,000, and $150,000 per QALY) answer the first question. Net Monetary Benefit (NMB = λ·ΔE − ΔC) answers the second.

Valuing health gains in dollars at the $100,000 per QALY benchmark and subtracting program bills shows that a 10-CHW team creates +$12.80 million in operating net value over 5 years (+$12.47M with startup, and +$2.46M to +$2.79M even at the strict $50,000 benchmark). Pairing Year 1 startup tables with 5-year cumulative ledgers shows county boards that the program stays high-value across every U.S. threshold.

Mode Classification (Mode A Cost-Effectiveness Model on Illustrative Cohort Assumptions): Program cost inputs ($2,500/client Year 1, $1,800/client ongoing, $55,000/yr amortized startup plus $50,000 Year 1 onboarding = $325,000 total startup; $1,930,000 Yr 1, $1,405,000/yr Yrs 2-5, $7,225,000 5-yr operating, $7,550,000 5-yr with startup), QALYs (40.0404252/yr, 200.202126 5-yr), hospital bed offset ($1,823,330/yr), ICERs ($48,202 Yr 1, $35,090 Yrs 2-5, $36,089 operating, $37,712 with startup), and net values are illustrative Mode A closed-form scenario calculations.

Average vs. Incremental Scenario Comparison: Comparing the illustrative 10-CHW scenario ($7,550,000 with startup for 200.20 QALYs) against usual care gives an average ratio of $37,712/QALY ($36,089 operating). Compared against an illustrative smaller 5-to-6 CHW scenario ($3,115,606 for 112.11 QALYs = $27,790/QALY), the incremental step-up adds +$4,434,394 for +88.09 QALYs (ICER = $50,339/QALY), following the incremental framework in Sanders et al. (2016) Second Panel on Cost-Effectiveness in Health and Medicine.

Common Questions & Method Notes

What is the difference between a "cost-effective" program and a "dominant (cost-saving)" program?
A program is cost-effective when it improves health (ΔE > 0) at an added cost (ΔC > 0) whose ratio (ICER = ΔC / ΔE) stays below the value benchmark (such as $36,089 to $37,712/QALY vs. $100,000/QALY). A program is dominant (cost-saving) only when it both improves health (ΔE > 0) and cuts net spending (ΔC < 0).
Why do analysts report Net Monetary Benefit (NMB) alongside the ICER?
Because the ICER is a ratio (ΔC / ΔE), it does not show total program scale, and a negative ratio can mean either a cost-saving plan (ΔC < 0, ΔE > 0) or a worse plan (ΔC > 0, ΔE < 0). Net value (λ·ΔE − ΔC) is linear, additive, and clear across all cases.
How much does Year 1 startup training change the 5-year cost per QALY?
In Year 1 alone, startup and ramp-up costs raise the ratio to $48,202 per QALY ($1,930,000 for 40.04 QALYs). Spread across the full 5-year horizon (200.20 QALYs), adding $325,000 in startup moves the ratio from $36,089/QALY ($7,225,000 operating) to $37,712/QALY ($7,550,000 total), well below the $50,000/QALY benchmark.
