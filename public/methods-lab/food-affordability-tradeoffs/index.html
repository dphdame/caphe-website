What Is Lost Across Poverty Measures?

Why Gross Income Cutoffs Miss High-Rent Food Strain—and When to Look at Income After Rent

Comparing a standardized four-person monthly food budget ($973/month) against 2019–2023 Census income and rent medians across four California communities shows where gross-income screens work and where rent reshapes the budget.

Why Primary Policy Fit Differs Across These Four Communities

A single statewide food-security intervention misfires when the underlying local constraint is different. In high-rent urban neighborhoods like Marin’s Canal District (Tract 1122.03), supermarkets are already close by (0.97 mi county mean) and median income sits at 220% FPL, so gross-income screens and new store subsidies miss the problem. Because 96.5% of households rent and median rent absorbs 42.1% of income, the most effective lever is adjusting for housing costs by ensuring eligible families claim the CalFresh excess shelter deduction alongside school nutrition and clinic-based Produce Rx programs that do not cliff at 130% or 200% FPL.

By contrast, Central Fresno (Tract 2) faces a dual constraint: low earnings ($3,103/month) push the post-rent food share to 46.4% in a county where 13.3% of tracts also lack nearby supermarkets, requiring sustained CalFresh benefit adequacy paired with mobile markets or transit links. Meanwhile, Nevada County (17.9% post-rent food share, but up to 12.4 miles to a SNAP retailer) needs rural EBT delivery and retailer onboarding rather than income-only screening, and Santa Clara County shows why health departments must target funding by census tract rather than countywide averages ($13,306/month) that mask renter distress.

Methodological Grounding, ACS API Record & Calculation Rules (Click to expand exact variables, GEOIDs, margins of error & formulas)

1. Why This Is a Modeled Comparison of Area Medians (Unit-of-Analysis Disclosure): In the U.S. Census Bureau's 2019–2023 American Community Survey (ACS) 5-Year tables, B19013_001E measures median household income across all occupied households in a geography, while B25064_001E measures median gross rent (contract rent plus utilities) across renter-occupied housing units. Subtracting B25064_001E from round(B19013_001E / 12) and comparing against a four-person food plan ($973/month) and four-person poverty guideline ($2,500/month) produces a modeled benchmark comparison at area medians—not an observed household budget for a single four-person renter family or a direct determination of individual CHIS survey eligibility. For reference, 2019–2023 ACS renter-occupied median household income (B25119_003E / B25119_003M) is $65,777/yr (±$16,576) in Marin Tract 1122.03, $29,155/yr (±$11,971) in Fresno Tract 2, $52,986/yr (±$8,277) in Nevada County, and $119,765/yr (±$1,703) in Santa Clara County.

2. Published Statutory & Survey Sources: (a) U.S. Census Bureau 2019–2023 ACS 5-Year Detailed Tables: B19013 (Median Household Income in the Past 12 Months, in 2023 inflation-adjusted dollars), B25064 (Median Gross Rent), B25003 (Tenure: total occupied units B25003_001E and renter-occupied units B25003_003E), and B22003 (Receipt of Food Stamps/SNAP in the Past 12 Months: total households B22003_001E and households receiving SNAP B22003_002E; note that B22003 measures household survey reporting of SNAP receipt over 12 months, not administrative caseload counts or eligibility take-up rates); (b) 2023 HHS Federal Poverty Guidelines: $30,000/year ($2,500/month) for a four-person household in the 48 contiguous states and D.C. (130% FPL = $3,250/month; 200% FPL = $5,000/month); (c) USDA Food and Nutrition Service (FNS) FY 2024 SNAP Cost-of-Living Adjustment & CNPP June 2023 Thrifty Food Plan: the June 2023 Thrifty Food Plan cost for the statutory four-person reference family (male and female ages 20–50, children ages 6–8 and 9–11) was $973.30/month, which USDA FNS rounded down to establish the Fiscal Year 2024 (Oct 2023–Sep 2024) 48-state maximum four-person SNAP/CalFresh allotment of $973/month; and (d) UCLA Center for Health Policy Research (CHIS): adult food security module universe restricted to households at or below 200% FPL through the 2023 cycle, expanded in 2025–2026 to 200–400% FPL.

3. Exact 2019–2023 ACS 5-Year API Record & Deterministic Calculation Rules: Monthly median income (Table 1, Row 1) is computed as round(B19013_001E / 12) to the nearest whole dollar. All subsequent dollar differences and percentage shares in the worked cards and Table 1 are computed directly from the displayed whole-dollar monthly lines (Row 1, Row 3, and Row 5 = $973):

Tenure (B25003): 292,557 / 654,467 = 44.7% renters. SNAP receipt (B22003): 37,459 / 654,467 = 5.7%.

4. Countywide SNAP Retailer Distance Methodology (Table 2, Row 3): Countywide grocery access metrics come from the CAPHE / California 58-county spatial food environment panel (county_rankings_ca_2023.csv, covering 9,039 California census tracts). For each census tract, Haversine great-circle distance was calculated from the 2020 Census tract population centroid to the nearest verified SNAP-accepting grocery store or supermarket (combining USDA FNS SNAP Retailer Locator records with multi-source store verification). Tracts are flagged as low-income/low-access using the USDA Economic Research Service (ERS) Food Access Research Atlas convention (tract poverty rate ≥20% and centroid distance >1.0 mile in urban tracts or >10.0 miles in rural tracts). Table 2, Row 3 reports countywide tract summaries: Marin County mean 0.97 mi, median 0.74 mi, 5 of 62 tracts (8.1%) low-income/low-access; Fresno County mean 1.45 mi, median 0.74 mi, max 15.53 mi, 30 of 225 tracts (13.3%) low-income/low-access; Nevada County mean 2.92 mi, median 1.98 mi, max 12.43 mi, 1 of 26 tracts (3.8%) low-income/low-access; Santa Clara County mean 0.60 mi, median 0.43 mi, 15 of 408 tracts (3.7%) low-income/low-access.
