Linked Federal and State Sanction Lists Identify More Excluded Medicaid Providers Than Federal Checks Alone

Linking paid Medicaid claims with federal exclusion files and state lists to check active providers.

A state or county team that audits Medicaid provider networks using raw directories instead of paid claims overstates clinic capacity and leaves older state sanctions unlinked. Linking 488,032 Medicaid billing providers (2018 to 2024) across paid claims, federal exclusions, and California suspension lists finds 487,384 active non-excluded providers and 648 sanctioned providers.[1] A federal-only check leaves out 199 state-suspended providers (30.7%), while 95 later-excluded fraud billers took in $9,694.90 per month unflagged.[2] Because formal exclusion trails final billing by a 50.0-month median gap, teams must pair federal and state list checks with paid-claims volume reviews.[3]

• Reconciliation Math (Mode B): 488,032 billing NPIs equal 487,384 non-excluded controls plus 648 sanctioned NPIs. The 648 sanctioned NPIs split into 299 Specification A financial fraud exclusions (204 dated 2018-2024 and 95 dated 2025 or later; statutes 1128(a)(1), 1128(a)(3), 1128(b)(1)). Another 199 are California S&I-only suspensions. The remaining 150 are other LEIE exclusions (patient abuse, license actions, controlled substances, and pre-2018 cases). The state-only share is 199 / 648 = 30.7%. The federal share is 449 / 648 = 69.3%.

• Payment Ratios & Enforcement Lag (Mode B): Median monthly paid compared to controls is 6,721.78 / 3,820.94 = 1.76x for in-panel cases. For post-panel cases, it is 9,694.90 / 3,820.94 = 2.54x. The enforcement lag is the historical median gap from a provider's final billing month to formal LEIE exclusion (50.0 months, mean 50.7, IQR 32.5-68.0). Refreshing registry linkages updates historical labels once exclusions post, but does not shorten OIG's investigation-to-exclusion timeline.

Why Counting Names on a List Differs From Measuring Active Billers

Provider directories and sanction files are both static lists. Each records a status on the day it was last updated. Read alone, the HHS Medicaid file lists 488,032 billing NPIs and 648 sanctioned providers as one sum. Linked to paid claims and to both federal and state lists, those 648 split into 299 fraud exclusions, 199 California-only suspensions, and 150 other cases.

Health economics treats list records as a data system that needs cross-checks on both sides. Directories keep listing providers who see few Medicaid patients. For example, Zhu et al. (2023) found 58% of listed Oregon Medicaid mental health providers saw few or no patients. Sanction lists also lag behind billing by years. The 95 providers excluded in 2025 or later billed a median $9,694.90 per month through 2024 without a flag. Formal exclusion arrived a median 50.0 months after final billing, so list lookups alone cannot flag active billing during that pre-exclusion window and must be paired with paid-claims volume checks.

Median monthly payments ($3,820.94, $6,721.78, $9,694.90), patient counts (33.42, 50.76), and the 50.0-month lag come from the same files. Common Questions & Method Notes

What does a single sanction list leave out in Medicaid provider data?
A single list records one status per provider as of its last update. In the linked 2018-2024 Medicaid file, 199 of 648 sanctioned NPIs (30.7%) appear only on the California DHCS Suspended and Ineligible list. A check of the federal LEIE alone does not return them.
Why do some sanctioned providers show up as active billers in claims data?
Formal exclusion lags behind billing. Among providers excluded for financial fraud, the median gap from the final billing month to the LEIE exclusion date is 50.0 months. The 95 providers excluded in 2025 or later billed a median $9,694.90 per month during 2018-2024 with no list flag.
How should analysts link directory, claims, and sanction records?
Match every NPI against paid claims, the federal LEIE, and the state suspension list. Record exclusion dates next to the last billed month, relink past claims when new bans post, and monitor high-volume paid claims during the 50.0-month window before formal exclusion.
