Standardized Healthy Life-Years and Federal Life Valuations Place Modeled Programs on Comparable Ledgers

Putting modeled chronic care, mental health aid, and saved lives into shared budget units.

Comparing county funding plans across blood sugar clinics, mental health aid, and disease control on separate chart scores leaves budget staff without a shared unit to rank health gains. Putting outcomes into common units shows that a modeled 750-client Santa Clara Community Health Worker cohort produces 40.04 healthy life-years per year ($4.00M at $100K per year), while county prevention saves lives at $109,000 each (125:1 return at $13.6M per life).[1] Relying only on lab tests undervalues mental health and social aid, which have no blood test yet bring 54.5% (21.83 of 40.04 healthy years) of modeled gains.[2] Keeping healthy years and saved-life values on separate ledgers avoids double-counting.[3]

How Shared Units Keep Upstream Care from Being Priced at Zero

In county health budgets, services with direct billing codes or lab blood tests easily win funding because their outputs are simple to count. Meanwhile, mental health aid, social-needs outreach, and countywide disease tracking face an uphill case. Without a shared unit, a budget office has no clear way to weigh a 20-person depression outreach team against a diabetes clinic or an environmental health unit.

Turning daily health gains into QALYs and saved lives into HHS Value of a Statistical Life (VSL) tiers levels the playing field. It shows that mental health and social outreach bring more than half (54.5%, or 21.83 of 40.04 QALYs/yr) of a CHW cohort's total quality-of-life gain. At the same time, countywide public health spending saves lives at $109,000 each, returning $58 to $190 in social value per $1 spent.

Mode Classification (Mode B Federal Schedules & LHA Slopes + Mode A Cohort QALY Scenario): HHS 2025 VSL tiers ($6.3M / $13.6M / $20.7M), U.S. value benchmarks ($50K / $100K / $150K per QALY), and the CAPHE LHA death slope (-9.16 per 100K per $10 spent) are Mode B facts. Cohort QALY parameters (+0.025/yr chronic, +0.040/yr mental health, +0.020/yr social needs across a 50%/30%/20% split at $1,800/client ongoing cost) and totals (40.04 QALYs/yr across 750 clients, or 0.0534 QALYs/client over 2 years at 3% discounting) are illustrative Mode A scenario assumptions.

Primary References & Data Sources: See U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) ASPE Guidelines for Regulatory Impact Analysis (2025 VSL Schedule) and U.S. EPA Mortality Risk Valuation ($11.2M VSL). We also use the Second Panel on Cost-Effectiveness in Health and Medicine (3% discount standard) and California DHCS SPA 22-0001.

Common Questions & Method Notes

What is a Quality-Adjusted Life Year (QALY) and how is it calculated?
A QALY combines length of life and daily health quality into one unit, where 1.00 is one year in full health and 0.00 is death. If an outreach team lifts a client's health score from 0.74 to 0.7675 (+0.0275 per year) for 2 years, it produces 0.0534 discounted QALYs at a 3% yearly discount rate.
When should analysts use QALYs ($50K-$150K/QALY) versus the Value of a Statistical Life ($13.6M VSL)?
QALYs and value benchmarks ($50K/$100K/$150K per QALY) are used in Cost-Effectiveness Analysis (CEA) to value daily health gains in care cohorts. VSL ($6.3M Low, $13.6M Central, $20.7M High) is used in Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA) strictly to value prevented deaths across a population.
How do we avoid double-counting when a client has both diabetes and depression?
Instead of adding two separate illness weights for the same person, assign one primary weight per client (50% chronic care at +0.025, 30% mental health at +0.040, 20% social needs at +0.020). Analysts can also use a single multi-symptom survey like the EQ-5D.
