Most Projected Medi-Cal Renewal Losses Stem From Preventable Paperwork Drop-Offs Rather Than Lost Eligibility

How our modeled renewal check splits form drop-offs among working and exempt adults from true income exits.

When reviewing Medi-Cal renewal drops, one county total leaves out whether adults lost eligibility or missed a form check.[1] In Santa Clara County, low jobless rates rule out a countywide waiver.[1] Yet 88.5% of Medicaid expansion adults already work or hold an exemption.[1][2] In our modeled four-group check, 52.5% of projected disenrollment comes from avoidable form drops (14,219 of 27,076 per cycle).[2][3] Flat state scaling projects 36,218 to 41,100 losses with no group split.[1][3] Splitting the funnel guides staff to exempt caregivers, disabled adults, and part-time workers.[3]

• Observed Pre-Policy Drift (DHCS Count): Santa Clara County expansion adult counts fell by -12.31% (-14,803 adults, from 120,239 in June 2025 to 105,436 in June 2026 in CHHS Open Data). That drop happened during routine unwinding before any work rule began.

• Zero Jobless Waiver Adjustment (Mode A): State DHCS funnel numbers subtract 373,389 adults in high-unemployment counties before paper checks (dhcs_verification_funnel_scc.csv:5). Santa Clara County has a low jobless rate (~4.1%). It gets a 0.0% county waiver share. Its paper check pool rises by +9,689 (from 72,437 to 82,126). At a 50% paper loss rate, county losses reach 41,100 per cycle instead of the flat-scaled 36,218.

• Phone Notice Upload Gain: Adding phone notice upload and form help stops 1,423 more DHCS-scaled losses (+3,131 on the ACS scale). Total stopped disenrollment rises from 14,219 to 15,642 of 27,076 (57.8%) on the DHCS scale. That equals 17,350 of 30,007 (57.8%) on the unscaled ACS base.

• Extended Cell Context. Work & Home Profile (Rule Exemptions). Parents (18,950), disabled adults (18,370), and students (10,210).

Scenario Calculations, Group Splits & ACS-Weighted Scale Crosswalk
Expand scenario notes

• Scenario 1 Base Counts: In the four-group model scaled to 120,239 DHCS adults, 54,152 reach paper renewal after auto checks. Of those, 27,076 (50.0%) lose plans each six-month cycle. That equals 30,174 on the 133,998 ACS 2019–2023 PUMS base, or 41,100 under flat state scaling with a 0.0% county jobless waiver.

• Scenario 2 Group Math: Auto clinic and school flags for exempt adults stop 6,831 modeled losses per cycle (1,241 caregivers + 2,295 disabled or new-parent adults + 3,295 students). Six-month hour checks for part-time workers stop 3,319 losses. Together they stop 10,150 losses (37.5% of 27,076 total losses, or 71.4% of the 14,219 avoidable pool).

• Scenario 3 Full Plan & Phone Upload: Adding wage follow-up for W-2 workers stops 2,228 losses. Clinic health checks and local service slots for non-working adults stop 1,841 losses. That brings the total to 14,219 (52.5%). Pairing with phone form upload adds +1,423 DHCS-scaled saves, reaching 15,642 of 27,076 (57.8%) on the DHCS scale (17,350 of 30,007 [57.8%] on the ACS scale).

How Funnel Decomposition Changes County Retention Budgeting

When a county sees a projected 36,000 to 41,000 drop in Medi-Cal plans, one top-line total gives no clear plan for staff. If leaders think those adults found job plans or chose not to work, the county will plan for smaller clinic panels. Meanwhile, ER teams will absorb thousands of newly uninsured patients who lost care over an unreturned form.

Splitting the renewal funnel by check stage and Census group shows that 88.5% of Santa Clara County's 120,239 expansion adults work at least 80 hours per month, hold a rule exemption, or work part-time hours. In our county model, 71.4% of all avoidable form losses (10,150 of 14,219 per cycle) hit exempt caregivers, disabled adults, students, and part-time workers. Moving outreach funds to clinic and school auto-flags, six-month hour checks, and one-page pay forms keeps people covered at a fraction of broad ad costs.

It is scaled to Santa Clara County's June 2025 DHCS base of 120,239 expansion adults (105,436 in June 2026; scc_dhcs_medical_enrollment.csv:1-134). The 133,998 ± 3,905 count (rounded to 134,000) is the ACS 2019–2023 5-Year PUMS weighted base (n = 6,297 records across PUMAs 08501–08515) used for group shares.

Common Questions & Method Notes

What share of Santa Clara County Medi-Cal expansion adults already work or qualify for an exemption?
In Census data scaled to the 120,239 DHCS base, 77.5% of expansion adults already work at least 80 hours per month (38.0%) or hold a caregiver, disability, or student exemption (39.5%). Another 11.0% work in self-employed or part-time roles, reaching 88.5% in all. Only 11.5% (13,767 adults) have no logged work or survey exemption.
Why does Santa Clara County face a higher per-capita disenrollment rate than the statewide DHCS average?
The state DHCS check funnel exempts 373,389 adults living in high-unemployment or disaster counties before paper checks. Santa Clara County has a low jobless rate (~4.1%). As a result, 0% of its adults get a countywide jobless waiver. Projected six-month losses rise from 36,218 (flat state share) to 41,100.
Which Medi-Cal groups experience the highest avoidable procedural churn?
Exempt caregivers, disabled adults, and students face 6,831 avoidable form losses per cycle (72.4% avoidable). Self-employed and part-time workers follow with 3,319 avoidable form losses per cycle (64.6% avoidable).
