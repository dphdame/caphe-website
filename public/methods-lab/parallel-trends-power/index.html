Trend-Drift Sensitivity Bounds Quantify How Much Pre-Policy Noise a County Evaluation Can Withstand

Testing how much post-policy trend drift a modeled county panel can take before its result crosses zero.

When auditing a county panel study that passes a pre-policy trend check, budget teams still face risk if wide pre-period noise bands let slow county drift pass unseen. In California's 57-county public health panel (1,197 county-years), the 9.16 fewer deaths per 100,000 finding holds across 46 of 47 model checks.[1] In a shorter 1,408-county panel, a -0.47 percentage point result holds up only if modeled post-policy drift stays below 35% (M* = 0.35) of pre-policy swing.[2] Passing a pre-trend check alone leaves a ±1.27 percentage point noise band (2.5 times the effect) unexamined, so reporting drift limits shows which findings can guide capital budgets.[3]

Why Passing a Pre-Trend Check Is Only the First Step

In county program reviews, analysts check that pre-policy lead points cross zero and cite a high joint p-value (such as p = 0.9997). Yet wide pre-period noise bands can let slow county drift pass that check. The smaller the sample or the wider the pre-period margin, the easier it is to pass a pre-trend check while an unseen slope accounts for the post-policy gap.

Modern health economics pairs pre-trend checks with explicit drift bounds. Reporting the Rambachan-Roth drift limit (M* = 0.35, a Mode A bound) and comparing short panels against 21-year models (such as the 57-county California LHA panel, N = 1,197 county-years, where F = 314.1 and 46 of 47 models hold) shows leaders how much trend drift a finding can take.

Mode Classification (Mode A Drift Bounds on Stated Base Inputs): Event-study pre-trend leads (-0.056, -0.030, 0.000), standard errors (0.620, 0.582, 0.549), and the base ATT (-0.47 pp, SE = 0.22, p = 0.036) are stated inputs from the 1,408-county DiD panel. The Rambachan-Roth limit (M* = 0.35) and its robust ranges are Mode A calculations on those inputs. California LHA splits (Pre-ACA -3.07 vs. Post-ACA -8.93; base-funding -4.56 vs. Full -9.16) come from the separate 57-county LHA panel (N = 1,197 county-years; N = 969 in model).

Common Questions & Method Notes

Why does a high pre-trend p-value (such as p = 0.9997) not prove that parallel trends hold?
A high p-value only means the data cannot rule out zero pre-trend. When pre-period standard errors are wide (0.55 to 0.62 pp), the 95% CI spans -1.27 to +1.16 pp. A drift slope 2.5 times larger than the -0.47 pp effect can still sit inside that band.
What does a sensitivity limit of M* = 0.35 mean for budget choices?
If post-policy trend drift exceeds 35% of the max slope change seen in the pre-period, the 95% robust range crosses zero and the finding is no longer statistically clear.
Why do big-county splits in California often look statistically noisy?
California has only a small count of very large urban counties. Restricting a county panel model to big cities cuts the cluster count, widens standard errors, and lowers statistical power even when the true effect is present.
