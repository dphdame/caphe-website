Per-Visit Medi-Cal Rates Cover Only a Small Fraction of County Living-Wage CHW Salaries

How flat Medi-Cal visit pay compares with county worker pay, modeled care savings, and added healthy years.

Two-Part Executive Opening · Paragraph 1: The Policy Setting · Paragraph 2: Analytical Setup & Headline Takeaway

Community Health Workers and Promotoras help California families manage chronic illness, schedule clinic care, and avoid emergency room trips. In 2022, Medi-Cal added a statewide per-visit billing benefit to help counties and clinics fund these workers.

When sizing a county Community Health Worker team, relying only on Medi-Cal visit billing leaves an 89% to 94% pay gap because one full-time CHW costs $92,684 to $122,102 per year across all 58 state counties while Medi-Cal pays a flat $31.74 per 30-minute visit.[1][2] In our four-model comparison, hospital and clinic models offset ~$107,000 to $291,000 in modeled Year-1 care costs.[1][3] Neighborhood outreach gains ~8.2 healthy life-years over two years at a modeled net cost of ~$7,800 per QALY.[3][4]

Class C Example 1 · before-after-trap (Evaluating Pre-Post Changes Against Baseline Trends)

Evaluating Pre-Post Changes Against Baseline Trends in Pre-Policy Drift

County Medi-Cal offices track monthly enrollment to see how new state or federal eligibility rules affect local coverage. When enrollment drops over a 12-month window, staff check how much of that change started before the new policy took effect.

When judging a program with a two-point before-and-after check, analysts can confuse an ongoing baseline trend with a new policy effect.[1][3] Across a large urban California county's 134-month Medi-Cal series (June 2015 to July 2026), expansion enrollment fell -12.31% (-14,803 adults) between June 2025 and June 2026 during routine post-unwinding renewals before any federal work rule began.[1][2] Splitting the multi-year series separates that pre-existing renewal drift from new policy effects.[2][3]

Class C Example 2 · p-hacking-multiple-testing (Testing Subgroup Drops With Multiple-Group Checks)

Testing Subgroup Drops With Multiple-Group Checks Across 20 Language Groups

County Medi-Cal outreach teams track monthly enrollment across 20 written language groups to place bilingual renewal navigators where families face the greatest coverage losses. In tiny language groups with fewer than 50 members, a few families moving can create huge percentage swings.

When ranking 20 language groups by raw percentage drop alone, small-denominator swings can pull bilingual staff away from the largest member losses.[1][2] In a multilingual California county's DHCS enrollment data (472,014 to 411,722 members, -60,292 total), Samoan (-34.04%, -16 members) and Chuukese (-25.00%, -1 member) show the steepest percentage drops.[1] Applying a multiple-group reliability check and minimum-volume floor directs bilingual outreach to Spanish (-24,880), English (-24,877), Vietnamese (-6,344), and Mandarin (-1,467), which account for 95.5% of county losses.[1][2][3]
