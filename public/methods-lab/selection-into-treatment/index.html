Wage-Tier Replacement Rates and Job Protection Shape Projected Paid Family Leave Take-Up

Modeling how higher pay rates and job protection shift leave claims across low-wage and high-wage workers.

When sizing a Paid Family Leave expansion from 8 to 12 weeks, using one statewide claim rate misprices fund reserves because low-wage and high-wage workers claim leave at different rates. Across California's 18.5 million covered workers, pairing a 12-week cap with 90% low-wage pay replacement delivers $422.0M per year in modeled net worker pay and a +$19.7M yearly net gain for the lowest-paid fifth of workers.[1] Assuming 100% of eligible claimants take every week overstates yearly fund outlays by 3.1 times ($1,910.0M vs. $623.7M) because job rules and firm top-offs shape take-up.[2] Modeling take-up across all five wage groups prices the payroll tax shift at +0.045 percentage points.[3]

• Scenario 1 Low Bound (25% Added Weeks / +5% New Claims): Adds +16,500 new claims (from 330,000 base to 346,500/yr). That creates $417.8M/yr in gross SDI outlay ($2.688B over 6 years, 1.33% SDI tax rate) and $201.8M/yr in net worker pay after 50% firm offset.

• Scenario 3 Full-Use Cap (100% Added Weeks / +20% New Claims): Adds +66,000 new claims (396,000/yr total). Gross outlay rises to $1,910.0M/yr ($12.286B over 6 years) and pushes the 2026 SDI reserve to a -$0.58B (-3.0%) deficit.

Why Worker Take-Up Shapes Both Causal Models and Tax Fairness

In program studies, selection into treatment means people who enroll differ from those who do not. For health economists and state budget leads, worker choice is also the policy path itself. California gives 12 weeks of unpaid job protection under CFRA, yet PFL pays for only 8 weeks. Low-wage workers without savings drop out at week 9, while salaried staff with firm top-offs take all 12 weeks.

Modeling worker take-up next to tax shares avoids two budget errors. It avoids over-budgeting for a 100% cap ($1,910.0M/yr) and avoids assuming leave expansions only help top earners. SB 951 raised low-wage pay to 90% and removed the wage tax cap on high earners. At 35% expected take-up ($623.7M/yr gross cost), the plan scores +0.095 on progressivity and sends +$23.0M in net gains to the bottom two wage groups.

Common Questions & Method Notes

Why does PFL take-up differ from total covered workers in California?
All 18.5 million SDI-covered workers pay into the fund. Yet low-wage workers face cash limits when pay replacement sits below 90%. They also face job-protection gaps when PFL pays for 8 weeks while CFRA protects jobs for 12 unpaid weeks.
How did Senate Bill 951 shift tax and pay shares in California Paid Family Leave?
SB 951 removed the SDI wage tax cap on January 1, 2024, so high earners pay tax on all wages. It then raised pay replacement to 90% for low-wage workers on January 1, 2025. Under a 12-week plan, Q1 (<$25K) workers gain +$19.7M net per year.
How does private firm offset affect SDI reserves versus worker income?
When firms use state PFL pay to offset private paid leave (30% in the Expected case), net new cash to workers drops from $602.8M to $422.0M. Yet the state SDI fund still pays out the full $623.7M gross outlay.
