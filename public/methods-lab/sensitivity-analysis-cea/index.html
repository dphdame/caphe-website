Modeled County Public Health Prevention Clears Cost-Neutral Thresholds Across All Federal Valuation Standards

Testing modeled returns across low, middle, and high federal life value tiers. Checking both point estimates and lower bounds.

When a county board reviews a prevention budget with one benefit rule, staff ask if the return holds under lower life values or smaller effect bounds. Across all three federal tiers ($6.3M, $13.6M, and $20.7M per life), California county public health spending (9.16 fewer deaths per 100,000) yields a modeled 58:1 to 190:1 return on the conservative $10 calculator scale (or 577:1 to 1,896:1 on the paper's $1 table scale).[1][2] Using one middle number (125:1 at $13.6M) leaves a plan open to debate over one input.[2] Showing that the lowest joint bound still yields 44:1 (440:1 on the $1 scale) shows that the spending pays off under every federal rule.[3]

Why the Goal of Sensitivity Analysis Is Decision Robustness, Not Parameter Perfection

County fiscal reviews test whether a public health investment remains cost-effective across conservative and upper-bound federal valuation tiers: should the analysis use the U.S. EPA $11.2M Value of a Statistical Life, the HHS $13.6M central benchmark, or the $6.3M HHS low tier? When an analyst presents only a single point estimate (125:1), any disagreement over one input casts doubt on the entire recommendation.

Sensitivity analysis reframes the conversation around which assumptions change the decision. Across the HHS 2025 VSL range, county public health spending returns 58:1 to 190:1 on the conservative $10 calculator scale (577:1 to 1,896:1 on the paper's $1 table scale), and the joint floor (lower confidence bound at $6.3M) still returns 44:1 (440:1 on the $1 scale), so the funding verdict does not depend on the VSL tier or unit scale. CHW cost capture behaves differently: direct FFS billing recovers 6% to 9% of a $112,915 position and payer offsets recover 95%, so for CHW programs the financing perspective, not the valuation tier, determines whether the budget closes.

Mode Classification & Unit Reconciliation (Mode A Scenario Grid on Observed and Published Inputs): The cross-multiplied scenario cells in Table 1 (44:1 to 190:1 on the $10 calculator scale, 440:1 to 1,896:1 on the $1 regression-table scale, model-implied Santa Clara $851M to $3.67B valuations that assume the statewide estimate applies locally, and CHW recovery ratios 0.06:1 to 2.58:1) are Mode A sensitivity calculations. Their inputs are observed econometric estimates (-9.158, SE = 1.108, 95% CI [-11.34, -6.98]), published HHS 2025 VSL schedules ($6.3M / $13.6M / $20.7M), the EPA VSL ($11.2M), and calibrated CHW cost and offset values from the CHW case study.

Common Questions & Method Notes

What is the difference between one-way and multi-way sensitivity analysis?
One-way sensitivity analysis varies a single parameter at a time while holding all other inputs at their central values (for example, varying VSL from $6.3M to $20.7M at the -9.16 effect size, yielding 58:1 to 190:1 on the $10 calculator scale or 577:1 to 1,896:1 on the $1 table scale). Multi-way sensitivity analysis varies two or more uncertain parameters together (such as combining the $6.3M Low VSL with the -6.98 lower 95% confidence bound to test a 44:1 conservative joint floor).
What is a "cost-neutral threshold analysis" in cost-benefit modeling?
Cost-neutral analysis asks how small the program effect would have to be for benefits to just equal costs (BCR = 1.0:1). At the $13.6M HHS Central VSL, a $10/capita investment reaches cost neutrality if it averts 0.0735 deaths per 100,000 residents (or 0.00735 per $1/capita), meaning the observed -9.16 effect is at least 125 times larger than the cost-neutral minimum.
Why are CHW cost-capture scenarios reported in separate rows from VSL-based returns?
The two measure different units. VSL-based returns value mortality reductions from county public health spending, while CHW cost capture compares cash or resource offsets with the $112,915 annual cost of one CHW position. Direct FFS billing recovers 6% to 9% of that cost and Medi-Cal payer offsets recover about 95%, so the CHW financing question depends on who captures the savings instead of on the VSL tier.
