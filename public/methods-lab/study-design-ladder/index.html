Each Public Health Budget Question Requires a Distinct Empirical Study Design

We match linked state records, trend splits, county panels, and outreach trials to each budget question.

In California public health budgeting, teams answer four separate questions on headcount, state law shifts, county returns, and outreach scripts that no single study design can solve alone. Matching each question to the right method locates 120,239 Medi-Cal members needing renewal outreach,[1] +39,600 Paid Family Leave claims,[2] 9.16 fewer deaths per 100,000 from county health spending,[3] and an 8% to 11% renewal lift from live calls.[4] Using one raw source alone undercounts Medi-Cal adults by -40.2% in home surveys or mixes trend growth with new policy shifts.[1][2] Pairing linked records, trend splits, county panels, and outreach trials gives leaders a full toolkit.[1][4]

Why Health Agencies Use All Four Methods Together

A county health director launching a Medi-Cal renewal or chronic-care plan needs four types of proof at four steps. First, record-survey linkage sizes the group (fixing the -40.2% ACS undercount to find 120,239 exposed adults and 26,515 LEP members). Second, trend models split baseline enrollment drift (-12.31%) from new laws. Third, 21-year county panels measure department returns (9.16 fewer deaths/100K, 125:1 BCR). Fourth, outreach trials test which contact channel works.

Checking each step of the delivery funnel turns study numbers into daily fixes. In California's 5-arm renewal outreach trial (N = 82,695, NBER WP 34191), changing the wording of texts and emails gave a 0% difference. Adding a live phone call lifted renewals by +8% to +11% (+1.0 to +1.8 pp). Likewise, splitting a CHW referral funnel shows that 78.1% of reached patients link to care. The 15.0% overall rate points to an early gap in starting referrals (27.8%), not a gap in CHW support.

Mode B Studies + Mode A PFL Simulation: Rung 1 (120,239 DHCS / 152,811 ACS vs. 255,601), Rung 3 (-9.16 routine-budget panel), and Rung 4 (NBER WP 34191 N = 82,695 and Bronx CHW 27.8% × 69.2% × 78.1% = 15.0%) are observed Mode B studies. Rung 2 (330,000 vs. 369,600 PFL claims, $623.7M) is a Mode A simulation calibrated to EDD forecasts. The four rungs use four separate groups (Santa Clara adult Medi-Cal members, California PFL claimants, 57 California counties, and Medicaid outreach and CHW trial participants). Their estimates are not pooled on one scale.

Main Sources: We cite NBER Working Paper 34191 (California Medicaid Renewal Outreach 5-Arm Trial, N = 82,695) and California DHCS Medi-Cal Enrollment Dashboards. We also use California EDD May 2026 DI Fund Forecast and Brown (2014), Health Economics.

Common Questions & Method Notes

Why does the ACS survey undercount Santa Clara County adult Medi-Cal members by 40.2%?
Home surveys face a known Medicaid undercount. People mix up Medi-Cal plan names (like Santa Clara Family Health Plan or Anthem Blue Cross) with private plans or leave out household members. Fixing totals to DHCS state records (255,601 adults vs. 152,811 in ACS) while using ACS for language and work-hour shares fixes the group base.
What did the 82,695-person California Medicaid outreach trial (NBER WP 34191) show about text wording vs. live calls?
Across four written arms testing different SMS and email wording, renewal lift was 0%. Only the fifth arm, which added a live phone call, raised renewals by 8% to 11% (+1.0 to +1.8 pp). Live human help clears paperwork hurdles where text tweaks do not.
Why does splitting a referral funnel into start (27.8%), reach (69.2%), and link (78.1%) matter for teams?
Looking only at the 15.0% overall completion rate (0.278 × 0.692 × 0.781) makes CHW outreach look weak. Splitting the funnel shows that CHWs link 78.1% of the patients they reach. The real operational gap is starting the referral (27.8%).
