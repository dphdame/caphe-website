Verified CalFresh Store Lists Double Measured Grocery Distance Compared With Commercial Map Directories

We compare raw business map lists with verified SNAP store lists when mapping local food access.

A county food team that switches its map from raw business listings to verified CalFresh EBT stores shifts local store counts even when zero stores opened or closed. Checking map lists against 514 verified CalFresh EBT stores and 755 county health permits keeps 81.7% of top-need areas (67 of 82 tracts) in the same top tier.[1][2] Raw map queries return 6,613 food spots (such as cafes that do not take CalFresh), so switching to verified grocers doubles mean trip distance by +97% (0.29 to 0.573 miles) with zero store closures.[1][2] Checking map feeds and 5-year census surveys against monthly permits and caseloads keeps grants aimed at real local gaps.[2][3]

Why Checking Data Rules Protects Both Program Checks and Grant Siting

Health teams often build dashboards from map APIs, store lists, permit logs, and rolling census surveys. Each source can change its own rule without notice. A map API drops a store tag (jumping from 344 to 6,613 spots). An EBT filter removes cafes (-92.2% store count, +97% distance). A permit registry lists bakery and deli counters inside one supermarket as separate rows (1,220 duplicates out of 8,555 rows, or 14.3%). Or a 5-year survey waters down a 1-year shock (-4.94% in 2020 ACS vs. +56% to +71% in state CalFresh rolls).

Teams should treat any sudden dashboard jump as a data-rule shift until checked against state or county rolls. Deduplicating county health permits by address (755 refined / 724 confirmed grocers at 97.5% accuracy), pairing 5-year census baselines (-1.4% gap vs. 2019 CalFresh admin rolls) with monthly caseloads, and checking rank stability (where 65.2% of tracts stay in the same quintile and 81.7% of top-need tracts stay in the highest-need tier) keeps county funds aimed at real local need.

Common Questions & Method Notes

Why did filtering from 6,613 broad spots to 514 SNAP stores keep 81.7% of top-need tracts (67 of 82) in the highest-vulnerability quintile?
In the 5-component Extended Vulnerability Index, economic factors (30% poverty rate and 25% SNAP participation) hold 55% of the index weight while normalized store distance holds 20%. Even though mean distance doubled from 0.29 to 0.573 miles, 65.2% of tracts (266 of 408) stayed in the exact same quintile and 81.7% of Q5 tracts stayed in Q5.
Why did the 2020 ACS 5-Year survey show a -4.94% dip in SNAP homes during COVID-19?
The 2020 ACS 5-Year table averages 60 months from January 2016 to December 2020, so 80% of the sample came before COVID-19. Also, 2020 survey collection faced low response rates among low-pay homes. Monthly CDSS rolls tracked the real +56% to +71% jump right away.
How did the County DEH health permit list check the store count and why was deduplication needed?
Raw Santa Clara County DEH permit logs contain 8,555 rows because supermarkets hold separate permits for bakery, deli, and meat counters at the same address (1,220 duplicate rows, or 14.3%). Consolidating by address (7,335 unique sites) and classifying retail grocers (97.5% accuracy) yields 755 refined / 724 confirmed SNAP-eligible grocers, proving the 6,613 map count was inflated 8.76x by non-grocery spots.
