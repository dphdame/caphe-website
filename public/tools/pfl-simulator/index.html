Postpartum Caregiving Timeline & Reverse-Calculated Infant Health Thresholds

Evaluates how adding 4 paid PFL weeks (8 → 12 weeks) shifts infant age at parental return to work relative to CDC routine immunization visits (2, 4, and 6 months) and quantifies the required effect sizes across 98,767 unique exposed infants statewide (4,593–5,446 in Santa Clara County).

Component A: Combined Postpartum SDI (6–8 Wks) + PFL (8 → 12 Wks) Caregiving Pathways

For birthing mothers, PFL bonding leave follows 6 weeks (vaginal) or 8 weeks (Cesarean) of pregnancy SDI recovery—meaning PFL weeks 9–12 occur at infant weeks 15–18 or 17–20, directly bridging the 4-month (~17.3-week) CDC immunization series. When two parents sequence leave, continuous parental care extends to infant weeks 28–32, bridging past the 6-month (~26.0-week) CDC immunization series.

Methodological Fencing (Identification Boundary): Literature estimates from California's 2004 PFL introduction (0 → 6 weeks; Huang & Yang 2015; Pihl & Basso 2019) and national reforms (Pac et al. 2023; Choudhury & Polachek 2021) capture the extensive margin of establishing paid leave at infant weeks 6–12. Because diminishing marginal returns may apply on the 8-to-12-week margin (infant weeks 14–20 for solo birthing mothers; weeks 20–32 for sequential two-parent households), this table reports reverse-calculated thresholds required for plausibility rather than unconditional causal point forecasts.
