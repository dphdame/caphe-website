Methodology & Econometric Model Specifications

Technical documentation for the California Paid Family Leave (PFL) 8-to-12-week expansion simulation model (Version 2026.2).

Core Purpose: This document specifies the primary-source administrative datasets, statutory benefit and solvency formulas, 2023 ACS earnings-bracket incidence parameters, postpartum caregiving timelines, and reverse-calculated public health outcome thresholds underlying the interactive simulator hosted on caphegroup.org.

1. Statutory & Policy Background

California workers currently face a four-week institutional gap between job-protected leave and state wage replacement:

  • California Family Rights Act (CFRA, Gov. Code §12945.2): Grants eligible employees at employers with five or more workers (meeting 12-month tenure and 1,250-hour thresholds) up to 12 weeks of unpaid, job-protected family bonding or caregiving leave.
  • California Paid Family Leave (PFL, Cal. Unemp. Ins. Code §3300 et seq.): Provides up to 8 weeks of partial wage replacement within any 12-month period.

Extending PFL from 8 to 12 weeks harmonizes wage replacement duration with CFRA job protection and aligns California with peer state social insurance programs (Washington, Colorado, New York, New Jersey, Oregon, and Massachusetts).

2. Financing Mechanism & Reconciled May 2026 EDD Solvency Trajectory

California PFL is financed entirely through employee payroll contributions to the State Disability Insurance (SDI) Disability Fund under Cal. Unemp. Ins. Code §984:

  • Taxable Wage Ceiling Removal (SB 951): Effective January 1, 2024, SB 951 repealed the SDI taxable wage ceiling, subjecting 100% of covered wages ($1,371.5B in 2026) to the 1.30% contribution rate.
  • Tiered Wage Replacement (§2655(d)(3)): Effective January 1, 2025, workers whose high-quarter wages are up to 70% of the State Average Quarterly Wage receive 90% wage replacement; higher earners receive 70% wage replacement subject to a statutory 90%-tier floor ($1,142.31/wk in 2026) and the maximum weekly benefit cap ($1,681 in 2025; $1,765 in 2026).
  • Gross Outlays vs. Employer Substitution (Crowd-Out): Because EDD pays the statutory PFL weekly benefit regardless of private employer top-up policies (unless an employer operates an EDD-approved Voluntary Plan), state DI Fund solvency and statutory tax rates depend on Gross Public Outlays ($623.9M in Expected 2026), whereas employer substitution (30% in Expected) scales Modeled Net Incremental Household Benefit ($422.0M).
  • Reconciled Reserve Adequacy Trajectory (2025–2030): Anchored to the EDD May 2026 DI Fund Forecast (Table 1: $3.011B ending 2025 balance [18.9%]; $3.484B 2026 forecast [19.9%] at 1.30%; $5.091B 2027 forecast [27.4%] at 1.40%), the Expected expansion scenario maintains positive cash reserves throughout 2025–2030 holding the 1.40% rate constant in 2028–2030, reaching $7.46B (35.4% reserve ratio) by 2030—comfortably inside EDD's 25%–50% operational reserve target range ($5.15B [24.4%] at 1.35% or $2.83B [13.4%] if rates revert to 1.30%).

3. Cost Modeling Framework & Weeks 1–8 Entrant Sensitivity

Gross Benefits = Baseline Claims × (1 + Extensive Margin) × Take-Up Rate × 4 Weeks × AWBA
Gross Public Outlays = Gross Benefits × (1 + 0.035 Admin Factor)
Modeled Net Incremental Household Benefit = Gross Benefits × (1 − Crowd-Out)

In the Expected 2026 scenario (330,000 baseline claims, 12% extensive margin = 369,600 expanded claims, 35% take-up = 129,360 modeled 4-week extension claim-equivalents, $1,165 AWBA), gross benefits equal $602.8M ($623.9M including 3.5% administration) and modeled net incremental household benefits equal $422.0M. Because the baseline formula prices only incremental Weeks 9–12, the simulator also provides explicit sensitivity bounds for Weeks 1–8 extensive-margin prior-week outlays (+$133.7M if only the 35% extension-using entrant subset draws 8 prior weeks; +$348.6M if all 39,600 entrants draw the 7.3-week baseline average).

4. 2023 ACS Earnings Brackets (B1–B5) & Distributional Incidence

Rather than relying on synthetic income quintiles, the simulator's equity module (02_analysis/05_equity_analysis.py) uses authentic earnings brackets from the U.S. Census Bureau 2023 1-Year ACS Table B20001 across 20,729,904 California workers with earnings (and 1,079,642 in Santa Clara County):

  • Bracket Definitions & Statutory Benefits: B1 (<$25,000; 26.3% of CA workers; $285.58/wk at 90%), B2 ($25,000–$44,999; 21.1% CA; $605.77/wk at 90%), B3 ($45,000–$64,999; 14.9% CA; $951.92/wk at 90%), B4 ($65,000–$99,999; 15.4% CA; $1,142.31/wk statutory floor = 74.2% effective replacement), and B5 ($100,000+; 22.3% CA vs. 41.0% in Santa Clara County; capped at $1,765.00/wk = 49.6% effective replacement at $185,000).
  • Dual Claimant-Participation Bounds: Case 1 (Historical Lower-Income Under-Utilization) weights claiming by historical pre-SB 951 propensities (0.45, 0.70, 0.95, 1.15, 1.10 across B1–B5); Case 2 (SB 951 + 12-Week Access Boost) models narrowed participation gaps under 90% replacement (0.80, 0.92, 0.98, 0.99, 0.85).
  • Calibrated Substitution-Adjusted Net Benefits: Bracket-specific employer substitution rates $c_b = \min(\theta \cdot \tilde{c}_b, 0.95)$ are calibrated so net benefits sum exactly to $421.97M and satisfy $\text{Net}_b < \text{Gross}_b$ in every bracket. Under Case 2, workers earning under $65,000 (B1–B3) contribute 27.1% of SDI payroll taxes but receive 39.6% of gross benefits and 48.1% of net incremental household benefits (Net Progressivity Index $\Delta C = +0.216$).

5. Postpartum Caregiving Timeline & Reverse-Calculated Health Thresholds

Implemented in 02_analysis/08_public_health_exposure.py, the public health exposure module links 2025 EDD observed claim-type shares (87.80% bonding = 113,582 Expected 2026 bonding claim-equivalents) to unique exposed infants using a 1.15 co-parent dual-claiming factor (98,767 unique exposed infants statewide, range 90,866–103,256; 4,593–5,446 in Santa Clara County based on 18,703 resident births in 2024 CDPH data and 78.55% maternal labor force participation in 2023 ACS Table B13012).

Required Per-Infant Risk Difference: Δp_req = Y* / N_infants
Required Effect per True Added Paid Bonding Week: β_week,req = Y* / (N_claims,bonding × 4 × π_time)

Across $\pi_{\text{time}} \in \{25\%, 50\%, 75\%, 100\%\}$, achieving +1,000 to +2,500 additional infants exclusively breastfed at 6 months requires +0.22 to +2.20 percentage points per true added paid bonding week (within the +1.0 to +1.7 pp/week historical reference range from Huang & Yang 2015), while avoiding 100 infant respiratory/GI hospitalizations requires a 1.22% to 4.89% relative risk reduction per added bonding week (within the 3%–6% historical reference range from Pihl & Basso 2019).
