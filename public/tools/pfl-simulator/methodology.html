Methodology & Econometric Model Specifications

Technical documentation for the California Paid Family Leave (PFL) 8-to-12-week expansion simulation model.

Core Purpose: This document outlines the empirical parameters, statutory solvency benchmarks, behavioral mechanisms, and data sources underlying the interactive simulator hosted on `caphegroup.org`. The analysis was developed by health economists to provide non-partisan, evidence-based fiscal forecasting for state policymakers, health advocates, and county health authorities.

1. Statutory & Policy Background

California workers currently face an institutional coverage gap between job-protected leave and state wage replacement:

  • California Family Rights Act (CFRA, Gov. Code §12945.2): Grants eligible employees up to 12 weeks of unpaid, job-protected family bonding or caregiving leave.
  • California Paid Family Leave (PFL, UIC §3300 et seq.): Provides up to 8 weeks of wage replacement (70%–90% of regular earnings).

This creates a four-week gap where workers have statutory job protection but zero wage replacement. The policy proposal modeled in this simulator extends PFL benefits from 8 to 12 weeks, harmonizing state wage replacement with CFRA duration.

2. Financing Mechanism & Operational Reserve Standards

CA PFL is funded entirely through the State Disability Insurance (SDI) employee payroll tax. Recent statutory reforms have altered the program's fiscal baseline:

  • Taxable Wage Ceiling Removal (SB 951): Effective January 1, 2024, the statutory cap on taxable wages subject to the SDI tax was removed, expanding the funding base to cover 100% of earned wages.
  • Tiered Wage Replacement: Effective January 1, 2025, SB 951 established a progressive benefit tier: workers earning below ~70% of the state average weekly wage (~$63,000) receive 90% wage replacement, while higher earners receive 70%, up to a statutory weekly maximum ($1,681 in 2025; $1,765 in 2026).
  • Reserve Adequacy Standard (UIC §984): The California Employment Development Department (EDD) defines 25% to 50% of annual disbursements as an adequate reserve rate target range under the statutory financing formula guidelines, not a mandatory statutory minimum. Under EDD's baseline May 2026 forecast without expansion, the SDI fund operates below 25% in 2025 (18.1%) and 2026 (18.2%). Under the Expected expansion scenario, the fund preserves positive cash balances throughout the projection period, reaching $6.37B (30.4% reserve ratio) by 2030, though its reserve ratio dips to 10.8% in 2026 before rebuilding.

3. Cost Modeling Framework & Behavioral Parameters

Cost projections utilize a micro-simulation framework that accounts for both the intensive margin (existing claimants taking longer leave) and the extensive margin (new claimants attracted by longer paid duration), adjusted for private employer substitution.

Net Expansion Cost = [Baseline Claims × (1 + Extensive Margin) × Take-Up Rate × 4 Weeks × AWBA] × (1 - Crowd-Out) × (1 + Admin Factor)

4. Worker Equity & Income Distribution Modeling

Distributional impacts across income quintiles were modeled directly in 02_analysis/05_equity_analysis.py using California wage profiles derived from the Census American Community Survey (ACS) 5-year series, parameterized across 18.5 million covered workers:

  • Quintile Wage Anchors: Q1 (<$25k, mean $18k); Q2 ($25k–$45k, mean $35k); Q3 ($45k–$70k, mean $57k); Q4 ($70k–$110k, mean $88k); Q5 (>$110k, mean $165k).
  • Replacement Tiering: Workers in Q1, Q2, and Q3 fall below the ~$63k threshold, qualifying for the 90% replacement rate under SB 951, while Q4 and Q5 receive 70% (capped at $1,710/wk).
  • Progressivity Finding: Workers in Q1 account for 7.0% of total taxable wages and SDI contributions, but capture 15.4% of expansion benefits, generating a net benefit increase of +$63.4M annually and an overall progressivity index of +0.095.

5. County Demographic Allocation Methodology

Standard public EDD monthly feeds publish aggregate statewide claim statistics. To provide local health departments, advocates, and county boards of supervisors with regional planning metrics, the simulator implements a deterministic demographic allocation:

  • Population Benchmark: Official county population counts from the California Department of Finance (DOF) demographic series and Census Bureau annual estimates across all 58 counties.
  • Deterministic Apportionment Formula: Each county's allocation share is calculated as $w_i = \text{pop}_i / \sum \text{pop}_j$. Statewide infant bonding claims (~85% of total PFL first claims paid) and net benefit dollars are allocated proportionally to each county by $w_i$.
  • Methodological Fencing: County counts represent allocated demographic projections, not raw EDD administrative claims. They are provided for local health planning and policy exploration.

6. Peer State Benchmarks

California enacted PFL in 2002 but has since fallen behind national peer programs on duration:

  • Washington (WA PFML): Provides 12–18 weeks at 90% wage replacement, funded via a shared 0.74% payroll contribution, with over 5 years of solvent operation.
  • Colorado (CO FAMLI): Implemented in 2024, providing 12–16 weeks at 90% replacement with a 50/50 employer/employee split (0.90% total).
  • New York (NY PFL): 12 weeks duration at 67% wage replacement (employee-funded).
  • New Jersey (NJ FLI): 12 weeks duration at 85% wage replacement.
